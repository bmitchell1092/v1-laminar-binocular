# Laminar profiles of visual response latency (spiking/MUA onset) in macaque V1

Scope note: this file covers the depth profile of spiking/MUA onset. Sink-versus-spike order at the input layer is already covered in `reports/CSD sink versus spiking onset order.md` and is not repeated. Most full texts (Maunsell & Gibson 1992, Nowak et al. 1995, Self et al. 2013, Gieselmann & Thiele 2022) could not be retrieved in this session (paywall, cookie wall or captcha). Where only an abstract or secondary summary was read, this is flagged. Figure numbers are given only where a retrieved text stated them.

## Q1. Which layers respond earliest, and by how much (layer-by-layer onsets)?

### Takeaway
Every classic single-unit and MUA study puts the earliest V1 spikes in the geniculate-recipient layers, which means 4Cα (magno) **and layer 6**, not 4C alone. Maunsell & Gibson (1992) found the shortest-latency units "all" in 4C or 6, at 20–31 ms. Supragranular onsets trail layer 4C by about 8–15 ms. 4Cβ trails 4Cα by roughly 5–20 ms, depending on study and stimulus. Recent dense-array work (awake and anesthetized, 2020–2025) keeps L6 in the "input" group, ahead of L2/3, 4B and L5.

### Cited Findings
- **Maunsell & Gibson 1992, J Neurophysiol 68:1332–1344** (single units, anesthetized macaques, 4 animals; abstract via secondary indexes, full text not read). Most V1 units had latencies of about 30–50 ms. The shortest latencies in each animal ranged from 20 to 31 ms, and "the units with the shortest latencies were all found in layers 4C or 6", with the most transient responses. Latency increased from layer 4 toward the surface, and the most superficial neurons became active about 15 ms after layer 4C. Magnocellular LGN lesions delayed the shortest latencies by 7–10 ms, so the first 7–10 ms of cortical activation was attributed to the M pathway. — [Bohrium abstract record](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/visual-response-latencies-in-striate-cortex-of-the-macaque-monkey/813071281883709440-8768); [PubMed 1432087](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1432087/)
- **Nowak, Munk, Girard & Bullier 1995, Vis Neurosci 12:371–384** (anesthetized, paralyzed; small flashing spots; abstract read). "The shortest latencies were found in layers 4Cα and 4B of area V1. Latencies in layer 4Cβ were on average 20 ms longer than those in 4Cα and 4B." The infragranular layers of V1 and V2 did not differ significantly. The abstract gives no layer 6 value. — [Cambridge abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/visual-neuroscience/article/visual-latencies-in-areas-v1-and-v2-of-the-macaque-monkey/2E43515431CBCFCC2451939F734E3427)
- **Schroeder, Mehta & Givre 1998, Cereb Cortex 8:575–592** (awake; diffuse flash; MUA and CSD; full text read). In V1, "Initial prominent responses occur in the major thalamo-recipient laminae, followed by responses in the extragranular laminae." The paper cites earlier work for an "8–15 ms" lag between the thalamorecipient and upper laminae (Maunsell & Gibson 1992; Givre et al. 1994) and quantifies the 4C and supragranular onsets per subject in Fig. 14. Upper and lower 4C sinks reflect M and P afferents respectively (citing Givre et al. 1995). The LGN-to-V1 lag is 10.9 ms (P < 0.05 in each animal). Areas and tracks were histologically verified. — [Schroeder et al. 1998 PDF](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~msereno/569/readings/CSD-visualareas.pdf)
- **Wang et al. 2020, J Neurosci 40:7436** (awake macaques, 1 h of laminar recording; Xing lab). Their Fig. 2 shows "a clear temporal order of response onset from input layers (L4C and L6) to output layers (L2/3, L4B, and L5)". Layers were aligned by CSD: 4Cα was placed at the earliest sink (relative depth 0.49) and the L5/6 boundary at a polarity inversion (relative depth 0.81). No histology was used. Table 1 gives only relative latencies, of a fast-suppression (FS) component against excitation: 11.35 ± 0.44 ms in L4Cα, 14.94 ± 1.25 in L6, and 18–21 ms in L2/3, L4B, L4Cβ and L5. L4Cα and L6 come first on this measure as well, and **L4Cβ (21.31 ms) is the slowest**. The stimuli were flashed 20 ms gratings at 90% contrast, 2 c/deg, at least 2.5× the RF, with onset at 2 SD above baseline. — [Wang et al. 2020 (PMC7511183)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7511183/)
- **Zhang, Li & Callaway 2024, eLife reviewed preprint 97290** (Neuropixels; 2 anesthetized/paralyzed macaques; full-field black/white and cone-isolating flashes). "In all penetrations the responses in L4Cα have the shortest latency." One S-ON example (Fig. 7B) responds "first in L4Cα, then L6A, followed by L4A, and finally L4Cβ". No millisecond values are reported. — [Zhang et al. 2024 (PMC10849622)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)
- **PNAS 2025 Neuropixels study** (2 anesthetized macaques; drifting Gabors; DiI tracks with histology, Fig. S1). It "validated that layer 4C neurons responded earlier than all other layers" (Fig. S3A). Section 2.6 is titled "Infragranular layers respond earlier to visual stimuli and precede activity in supragranular output layers". No millisecond values appear in the text, and L5 and L6 are pooled. — [PMC12132580](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12132580/) (author list not captured; verify before citing)
- **Bijanzadeh et al. 2018, Neuron** (anesthetized; MUA; 24-channel arrays at 100 µm). The absolute L4C spiking onset to RF-sized gratings was **23 ms** (Table S6). The RF-stimulation ΔL for L4C was 3.5 ± 1.35 ms, the shortest of all layers. Its "DeepL" (≈L6) group was the latest for surround suppression onset (for example, full-surround ΔL 32.17 ± 4.06 ms against 4 ± 1.15 ms in L4C; Fig. 7B). That is suppression timing, not the onset of the driven response. — [Bijanzadeh et al. 2018 (PMC6279245)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279245/)

### Inferences
- The consensus order is 4Cα ≈ (part of) L6 < 4B ≲ 4Cβ < L2/3 and L5. Supragranular layers lag by about 8–15 ms. 4Cβ is not reliably earlier than layer 6.
- Layer 6 joining the earliest group is a replicated observation across Maunsell & Gibson 1992 (units), Wang 2020 (awake, CSD-aligned) and Zhang 2024 (6A ahead of 4Cβ). It is not an anomaly.

### Gaps
- No retrieved source gives absolute layer-by-layer onset means (in ms) with SEMs for all six layers in one study. The values must live in the figures and tables of Maunsell & Gibson 1992 (likely the latency-by-depth scatter) and Nowak et al. 1995, which were not readable here.
- Self et al. 2013 (Curr Biol), Xing et al. 2012, Cox et al. 2019, Kajikawa, Hawken/Shapley (Ringach et al. 1997 says only that tuning emerges 30–45 ms after stimulus onset), Trautmann et al. (NHP Neuropixels) and Chen et al. were not found with laminar latency values in this pass. Westerberg et al. 2019 is covered in the earlier report. Nandy et al. 2017 is V4, not V1.

## Q2. Is 4Cα earlier than 4Cβ? Is layer 6 (6A/6B) as early as 4C? Where do the earliest onsets sit relative to the 4C/5 boundary?

### Takeaway
Yes, 4Cα leads 4Cβ. The estimates are about 20 ms for flashed spots in anesthetized animals (Nowak 1995), 7–10 ms by M-lesion inference (Maunsell & Gibson 1992), about 10 ms at the level of the LGN (Maunsell et al. 1999) and 5–6 ms for MUA in Schroeder et al. (1989, as cited). Some L6 sites are as early as 4C, and Zhang et al. (2024) place L6A before 4Cβ. No study retrieved here reports the earliest onset depth in µm relative to the 4C/5 boundary.

### Cited Findings
- 4Cβ lags 4Cα and 4B by about 20 ms on average, attributed to M being activated about 20 ms before P. — [Nowak et al. 1995](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/visual-neuroscience/article/visual-latencies-in-areas-v1-and-v2-of-the-macaque-monkey/2E43515431CBCFCC2451939F734E3427)
- In the LGN, "the fastest magnocellular response latencies preceded the fastest parvocellular response latencies by about 10 ms" over a wide intensity range (78 M and 80 P cells). The authors warn that convergence in cortex could erase or reverse the M advantage, which "calls into question inferences ... about ordinal relationships of neurons based on timing". — [Maunsell et al. 1999, Vis Neurosci 16:1–14](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/visual-neuroscience/article/abs/visual-response-latencies-of-magnocellular-and-parvocellular-lgn-neurons-in-macaque-monkeys/8AB0A24431EF71A91229FA28E272EC12)
- Schroeder et al. (1989) are cited as reporting a 5–6 ms M–P multiunit latency difference. Secondary citation; the original was not read. — [Maunsell et al. 1999 via ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/13265977_Visual_response_latencies_of_magnocellular_and_parvocellular_LGN_neurons_in_macaque_monkeys)
- Zhang et al. (2024) attribute the latency step at the 4Cα/4Cβ border to "the faster conduction velocity for magnocellular afferents to L4Cα versus parvocellular to L4Cβ". Where there is "a clear latency transition in the middle of L4C… this is usually marked as the L4Cα/L4Cβ border". The L4C/L5 border is set from AP power and coherence, **not latency** (Figs. 7, 8). — [Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)
- Geniculate projections to layer 6: M axons reach 4Cα and **lower layer 6**, and P axons reach 4Cβ and **upper layer 6** (review). — [Briggs & Usrey 2011, J Physiol 589:33](https://www.cns.nyu.edu/~tony/vns/readings/briggs-usrey-2011.pdf). M and P LGN axons end mainly in 4C, "with a lighter projection to layer 6". — [Briggs & Usrey 2007, J Neurosci 27:5431](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/27/20/5431)
- Layer 6 corticogeniculate cells (somata in layer 6 and the adjacent white matter) can have very short combined response and feedback latencies, with the shortest about 37 ms. — [Briggs & Usrey 2007](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/27/20/5431)
- Wang et al. (2020) put L6 (14.94 ms) second only to L4Cα (11.35 ms) on their relative FS latency, and ahead of L4Cβ (21.31 ms) (Table 1). — [Wang et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7511183/)

### Inferences
- In awake recordings with high-contrast gratings, an M–P difference at the 4Cα/4Cβ step of about 5–10 ms is more plausible than the 20 ms reported for small flashed spots under anesthesia. Spot stimuli and anesthesia favour slow P onsets.
- The project sees earliest spiking 0.3–0.7 mm below an input/deep boundary marker. If that marker is at or near the bottom of 4C, layer 5 lies just below it (roughly 0.1–0.25 mm, from general anatomical knowledge; not verified in this pass), followed by layer 6 and then white matter. A 0.3–0.7 mm offset would then land in layer 6, most likely lower 6 (M-recipient), or in the gray/white transition. The literature expects early spiking there: Maunsell & Gibson's earliest units were in 4C **or 6**. The bottom of that range (0.6–0.7 mm) is more likely white matter, where afferent axon spikes are expected (Q3).
- If the marker instead sits at the top of 4C or in mid-4C (for example the earliest-sink centre), 0.3–0.7 mm below it would fall in 4Cβ, L5 or upper L6. L5 is never reported as earliest, and 4Cβ is reported as late, so an earliest onset there would argue for a misplaced marker or for an afferent-axon contribution.

### Gaps
- No study found gives a µm-resolved latency profile across the 4C/5/6 boundary with histological layer assignment. Maunsell & Gibson (1992) had lesion-based reconstruction, but its depth plots were not accessible.
- No source compared the onsets of L6A and L6B directly.

## Q3. Do MUA recordings pick up thalamocortical afferent axon spikes (in 4C and in the white matter / layer 6) that make deep sites look early?

### Takeaway
Yes, qualitatively. White matter below V1 carries visually driven MUA with a flat CSD (Schroeder 1998). Axonal (positive or inverted) spikes are recorded in the white matter and in 4Cβ on Neuropixels (Zhang 2024; PNAS 2025). LGN afferents can be isolated within 4C and 4A once the cortex is silenced. No macaque study quantifies how much afferent spiking advances the onset of intact-cortex MUA by depth.

### Cited Findings
- "Active white matter regions can be characterized, therefore, by multiunit activity in the presence of a near-zero second derivative." At the grey/white boundary, "multiunit activity often extends considerably beyond this point". MUA with a flat CSD was used to identify white-matter spans (Methods; Fig. 4A shows white-matter MUA below each V1 belt). — [Schroeder et al. 1998](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~msereno/569/readings/CSD-visualareas.pdf)
- On Neuropixels, inverted spikes "measured from axons in the white matter" mark the L6/WM border (Fig. 4B). "Some inverted spikes appear in L4Cβ, where parvocellular afferents are dense." — [Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)
- Positive-spiking waveforms were "more common in white matter" (Fig. 1F; χ²(1, 95) = 41.779, p < 0.0001) and are "likely axons passing through the white matter" (Section 2.7; Fig. S7). — [PNAS 2025, PMC12132580](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12132580/)
- Single LGN afferent axons have been recorded in muscimol-inactivated macaque V1: red/green afferents in lower 4C, and blue/yellow in 3B/4A. — [Chatterjee & Callaway 2003, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature02167). Afferents with weak and strong surround suppression "were found throughout cortical input layers 4C and 4A". — [Sceniak et al. 2006, J Neurophysiol (PubMed 16928793)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16928793/) (abstract-level)
- Mechanism (cat, labelled as such): 182 geniculate afferents were recorded as units throughout layer 4 after muscimol. — [Jin et al. 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2556869). The earlier project report covers the terminal-to-sink interval of about 0.7 ms.
- LGN onset precedes V1 onset by 10.9 ms (CSD/AVREC; awake; diffuse flash). — [Schroeder et al. 1998](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~msereno/569/readings/CSD-visualareas.pdf)

### Inferences
- Afferent axon spikes reach V1 earlier than the cortical spikes they drive, by roughly a synaptic delay plus integration time. On the LGN–V1 gap, that is up to a few ms and at most about 10 ms. In the white matter and lower L6, where M afferents run and arborize, analog MUA could therefore lead 4C cortical spiking by a few ms with no cortical cell firing early. This is a plausible account of "earliest spiking 0.3–0.7 mm below the boundary". It is supported by the qualitative evidence above, but its size is unmeasured.
- A test with existing data: the putative afferent component should show positive or triphasic waveforms in the sorted units at the deepest contacts, little CSD (a flat second derivative), short-latency transient responses, and weak adaptation across repeated presentations (see the earlier report's repetition test).

### Gaps
- I found no macaque study that separates presynaptic from postsynaptic contributions to MUA latency by depth, or that gives the white-matter MUA onset relative to 4C MUA onset in ms.

## Q4. How does latency depend on contrast, size, animal and eccentricity?

### Takeaway
Lower contrast lengthens V1 latency. This is well established qualitatively, but the classic values (Gawne et al. 1996; Albrecht et al. 2002) could not be verified here. Large surrounds add late suppression whose onset differs strongly by layer (Bijanzadeh 2018). Laminar latency profiles split by eccentricity or monkey were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Contrast changes affect both the magnitude and the latency of responses, and lower contrast delays processing in retina, LGN and V1. These are secondary statements from search snippets, not primary values. — [PMC6619067](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6619067)
- Confirmed citations for the primary sources (values not verified): Gawne, Kjaer & Richmond 1996, J Neurophysiol 76:1356–1360, "Latency: another potential code for feature binding in striate cortex"; Albrecht, Geisler, Frazor & Crane 2002, J Neurophysiol 88:888–913, "Visual cortex neurons of monkeys and cats: temporal dynamics of the contrast response function". — [search record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/52499DC532E7CB19AEEDAEB0B7AFABD5)
- M-to-P LGN latency difference is about 10 ms "over a wide range of stimulus intensities". — [Maunsell et al. 1999](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/visual-neuroscience/article/abs/visual-response-latencies-of-magnocellular-and-parvocellular-lgn-neurons-in-macaque-monkeys/8AB0A24431EF71A91229FA28E272EC12)
- Surround suppression onset differs by layer: L4C 4 ± 1.15 ms, upper L4 13.8, L2/3 22.7, DeepU (≈L5) 16.8 and DeepL (≈L6) 32.2 ms (ΔL, full 20° surround; Fig. 7B). Far-surround suppression reached L4C late (42.5 ± 12.7 ms) but DeepU early (8.2 ± 2.7 ms) (Fig. 7E). — [Bijanzadeh et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279245/)
- Schroeder et al. (1998) show per-subject 4C and supragranular onset means (Fig. 14) and report consistent area-level lags within animals. — [Schroeder et al. 1998](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~msereno/569/readings/CSD-visualareas.pdf)

### Inferences
- For the project's paradigm (gratings at 45% vs 90% contrast), the contrast-driven latency shift is likely several ms (from general knowledge; not verified) and probably larger in P-dominated 4Cβ than in M-dominated 4Cα and L6. Comparing the latency *profile* at the two contrasts could therefore help separate M-recipient sites (small shift) from P-recipient sites (larger shift). M cells saturate at low contrast and P cells do not.
- Grating size affects onset mainly through surround suppression, which comes after the onset of the driven response. Size should barely move the half-max onset but can move peak-based estimates.

### Gaps
- Contrast-latency slopes by layer in macaque V1 were not found.
- No source broke down laminar latency profiles by eccentricity or by monkey.

## Q5. Has the latency profile (earliest MUA) been used as a laminar landmark, and how well did it agree with CSD or histology?

### Takeaway
Yes, but mostly as a secondary or within-4C marker. Zhang et al. (2024) use latency transitions to place the 4Cα/4Cβ border, and explicitly not the 4C/5 border. Ferro et al. (2021) define layer IV by the earliest sink *and* the shortest MUA latency. Wang et al. (2020) anchor on the earliest sink. None of the retrieved papers validates the earliest-MUA landmark against histology at µm precision.

### Cited Findings
- Zhang et al. 2024: "When there is a clear latency transition in the middle of L4C… this is usually marked as the L4Cα/L4Cβ border." If there is none, the border is placed at mid-4C. ΔP/P latency is "more reliable and yields sharper transitions than ΔCSD profiles". For L4B, "their latencies are expected to be longer than that of L4C". The L4C/L5 border comes from AP power spectra and coherence. The method is designed to work "without the assistance of postmortem histological staining", and latency is not validated against histology. — [Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)
- Ferro et al. 2021 identify layer IV by "the earliest current sink of the CSD and the shortest multiunit response latency" (quoted in the earlier project report). — [Ferro et al. 2021 (PMC8000025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8000025/)
- Wang et al. 2020 anchor 4Cα on the earliest sink, then classify L4C and L6 as "input layers" by response-onset order. No histology. — [Wang et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7511183/)
- Bijanzadeh et al. 2018: L4C is the earliest sink; the L6/WM boundary is "the deepest contact where spike rates dropped >50%". Other boundaries were estimated from anatomy (Lund 1973) and checked with DiI and cytochrome oxidase histology. The authors call the boundaries "necessarily tentative". — [Bijanzadeh et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279245/)
- Schroeder et al. 1998 used MUA with a flat CSD to locate white matter and track reconstruction for laminar assignment. — [Schroeder et al. 1998](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~msereno/569/readings/CSD-visualareas.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Earliest MUA" is an ambiguous landmark in macaque V1, because two layers (4Cα and L6) and the afferent-carrying white matter all qualify as early. On its own it cannot locate the 4C/5 boundary. The most defensible use is as a profile: a first early band (4Cα), a later band (4Cβ), a dip (L5), a second early band (L6) and MUA with a flat CSD (WM). The 4C/5 boundary is then the trough between the two early bands, cross-checked against the CSD sink bottom, AP power and coherence (Zhang 2024) and the project's SPI/PPC marker.
- If the project's earliest spiking sits 0.3–0.7 mm below its marker, the two-band reading predicts a latency trough (L5) between the marker and the earliest site. If no trough appears, the deep early site may be the 4C band itself, which would mean the marker sits too high.

### Gaps
- I found no study that reports quantitative agreement (µm error) between a latency-defined boundary and histology or CSD.
