# Where the initial visually evoked CSD sink lies in macaque V1 relative to histological layers, and how far the 4C/5 boundary is below it

Scope: anatomy and position only. Timing of the sink against spiking is covered in `reports/CSD sink versus spiking onset order.md` and is not repeated here. Overlap with the sibling note `lfp_markers.md` (Q4: Maier 2010, Zhang/Li/Callaway 2024, Mendoza-Halliday 2024 identifiability) is cross-referenced rather than duplicated. Researched 2026-11-12.

## Q1. Which studies checked CSD landmarks in primate V1 against anatomy, and what did each find?

### Takeaway
Very few macaque V1 studies compared the CSD sink against histology in the same penetrations, and **none reports a measured µm distance between the sink (top, centre or bottom) and a histological 4C/5 border**. Two kinds of validation exist. The Schroeder/Vaughan lineage (Kraut 1985; Schroeder 1991, 1998; Givre 1994/95) correlated lesions and track reconstructions with VEP inversion depths and CSD. Anaesthetised studies (Smith 2013; Bijanzadeh 2018; Zhu 2026) used lesions, DiI and cytochrome oxidase (CO) to check verticality and overall thickness, then placed 4C by CSD and the other borders by anatomical proportions. Everyone else cites these as "histologically verified" and applies the sink rule without histology of their own.

### Cited Findings
**Schroeder/Vaughan lineage (awake macaque, diffuse flash or pattern; lesions and Nissl track reconstruction)**
- **Kraut et al. 1985** (flash VEP, unanaesthetised monkeys, abstract only): the surface N24 and N40 are generated "within laminae IVA and IVCb respectively, both parvocellular thalamorecipient layers". MUA from 20–60 ms is maximal in IVA and "concurrent with the sinks observed within the parvocellular thalamorecipient sublaminae IVCb and IVA". The abstract does not mention lesions. Schroeder 1998 later cites Kraut 1985/1991 as part of its "confirmation of VEP component inversion depths from reconstruction of electrode tracks and lesions". — [Kraut et al. 1985, EEG Clin Neurophysiol (PubMed 2408876)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2408876/); [Schroeder et al. 1998](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~msereno/569/readings/CSD-visualareas.pdf)
- **Schroeder, Tenke, Givre, Arezzo & Vaughan 1991** (pattern-reversal gratings, abstract only): "N50 is generated primarily by current sinks in Lamina 4C". The sequence is "initial activation of the thalamorecipient subdivisions of Lamina 4C, followed by activation of mid/upper Lamina 4 and of supra- and infragranular laminae". **The abstract does not name 4Cα.** — [Schroeder et al. 1991, Vision Res (PubMed 1891808)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1891808/)
- **Schroeder, Mehta & Givre 1998** (diffuse flash, 14-contact arrays at 100 or 150 µm):
  - Methods: penetrations were "reconstructed in postmortem histology"; "During some electrode penetrations, lesions were made by passing current through one electrode contact". Sections of 40–120 µm were stained with potassium ferricyanide and cresyl violet.
  - The V1 landmark was not "bottom of the sink". It was a VEP inversion: "the center of the electrode array was offset slightly above the base of lamina 4c as defined by the inversion point of the flash-evoked N40 component (see Schroeder et al., 1991)".
  - Laminar assignments "could be made with confidence … because of our prior, extensive confirmation of VEP component inversion depths from reconstruction of electrode tracks and lesions (… Kraut et al., 1985, 1991; Givre et al., 1994)".
  - Outer borders: "the outer boundaries of an active cortical region, both pial and white matter, are indicated by drops to near-zero CSD values". Internal assignments were "approximate", "based on the known internal geometry of the region".
  - Fig. 3 (100 µm spacing) shows the electrode scaled onto a CO section. Fig. 2 shows Nissl tracks and lesions. **No µm offsets between sink and anatomy are reported.**
  - [Schroeder et al. 1998, Cereb Cortex 8:575–592](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~msereno/569/readings/CSD-visualareas.pdf)
- **Givre et al. 1995** (full-field coloured vs white flashes, abstract only): current flow was enhanced "in both layer 4c and the supragranular laminae". Schroeder 1998 summarises the paper's laminar result: "The stimulus sensitivities of the upper and lower Lamina 4C sinks show that they receive input from M- and P-type thalamic afferents respectively (Givre et al., 1995)". — [Givre et al. 1995, Vis Neurosci (PubMed 7786845)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7786845/); [Schroeder et al. 1998](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~msereno/569/readings/CSD-visualareas.pdf)
- **Givre et al. 1994** (V1 and V4, abstract): this is the V4 lesion-alignment study. Schroeder 1998 states that its alignment used "both lesions and depth relative to the pial surface". — [Givre et al. 1994, Vision Res (PubMed 8303826)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8303826/)

**Electrical stimulation (macaque, for laminar targets of the afferent streams)**
- **Mitzdorf & Singer 1979** (optic-radiation/LGN electrical stimulation, CSD, abstract): "The fast afferents induced monosynaptic activity in layer IV C alpha and layer VI". "The slow [afferents] induced monosynaptic activity in lower layers IV C beta and layer VI." This is the paper Maier 2010 cites for "4Cα". — [Mitzdorf & Singer 1979, J Comp Neurol (PubMed 114553)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/114553/)

**Anaesthetised studies with histology**
- **Smith, Jia, Zandvakili & Kohn 2013** (anaesthetised *M. fascicularis*, V1):
  - "Our CSD analysis provided clear sinks in six of the eight recorded penetrations … confined to one to two recording sites, consistent with the thickness of layer 4C (Fitzpatrick et al., 1985; Lund, 1988)."
  - "As a third method … we made and recovered electrolytic lesions in three of the eight penetrations", placed "at the final recording sites and again 200–300 µm into the WM". "In each case, the recovered lesions were at the expected locations" (Fig. 1C, Nissl section).
  - With alignment to the L6/WM border, the weak-correlation zone sat "roughly 0.8 mm above the layer 6/WM border", which matches "the expected depth and width (300–400 µm) of layer 4C".
  - The middle-layer sink was defined as the depth/time first reaching 40% of the CSD minimum and followed by a source.
  - The lesions mark the array ends, not the sink. The test is coarse: it checks consistency, not distance.
  - [Smith et al. 2013, J Neurophysiol 109:940 (author PDF)](https://jiaxx.github.io/assets/img/2012%20Laminar%20dependence%20of%20neuronal%20correlations%20in%20visual%20cortex.pdf); [journal page](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/jn.00846.2012)
- **Bijanzadeh, Nurminen, Merlin, Clark & Angelucci 2018** (anaesthetised, 24-ch at 100 µm, 10 perpendicular penetrations in 4 macaques):
  - Probes were DiI-coated; "DiI-labeled tracks were visualized under fluorescence to ascertain verticality". "Adjacent tissue sections were counterstained for cytochrome oxidase for identifying cortical layers" (Fig. 2B).
  - "input L4C can be identified as the location of the earliest current sink followed by a reversal to current source in response to RF stimulation (Fig. 2C) (Schroeder et al., 1998)". The stimulus was a flashed 0.5° black square over a 3×3° field.
  - "The boundaries between other layers were estimated based on previous anatomical studies (Lund, 1973) and postmortem histology, therefore, these boundaries are necessarily tentative."
  - "All our penetrations spanned cortical depths of 1.5–1.7mm, and the bottom of L4C across penetrations was consistently located at depths of 0.95–1.1mm from the top."
  - "Upper-L4" runs from the top of L4C to "approximately 1–2 contacts above the top of 4C", and "likely encompassed layers upper-4Cα and 4B, and possibly L4A".
  - Discussion: "Self et al. (2013) overestimated the thickness of L4C and underestimated that of the superficial layers, compared to our layer estimates, which were based on anatomy and postmortem verification of laminar thickness". The authors also note that averaging across aligned penetrations "can lead to layer misalignments".
  - **They do not separate 4Cα from 4Cβ, and give no sink-to-boundary µm value.**
  - [Bijanzadeh et al. 2018, Neuron 100:259 (PMC6279245)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279245/)
- **Zhu et al. 2026** (Angelucci lab, eLife, Neuropixels in V1, DiI derivative FM1-43FX):
  - "We located the lower boundary of the major sink (the reversal point of sink and source) as the border between layer 4C and layer 5/6."
  - "We also considered anatomical data … allowing for minor adjustments (±1 group channel) in layer boundary placement. Subsequent layer boundaries were determined by offsetting the cortical thickness derived from histological slices" (Fig. 1—figure supplement 1).
  - Mean compartment thicknesses were "5/6, 4C, 4A/B, 2/3 … 489, 281, 311, 650 µm, respectively".
  - **No discrepancy statistic between the CSD border and histology is given.**
  - [Zhu et al. 2026, eLife RP103255 (PMC12774416)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12774416/)
- **Zhang, Li & Callaway 2024** (Neuropixels at 20 µm, no histology of their own): they derive borders from AP-band metrics checked against published anatomy and argue that "the boundaries between sinks and sources of CSD profiles do not necessarily correspond closely to laminar borders using anatomical features". Details are under Q2–Q4 and in the sibling note. — [Zhang et al. 2024 (PMC10849622)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)

**MRI-only localisation (Maier/Westerberg lineage)**
- **Dougherty, Cox, Westerberg & Maier 2019** (J Vis):
  - Tracks were "traced via anatomical MRI", and the sink method is called "well-established and histologically verified". The authors cite Cox 2017, Dougherty 2015, Maier 2010, Maier 2011 and Mitzdorf 1985, none of which is itself a histology study.
  - "The bottom of this sink was used as a marker of the transition between granular Layer 4C and the deeper Layer[s]."
  - Across n = 41 penetrations, "the bottom of the granular Layer 4 was located 1.2 mm from the top of V1 and 0.5 mm from the bottom of V1" (Fig. 1D).
  - [Dougherty et al. 2019, J Vis 19(13):1 (PMC6797477)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6797477/)
- **Westerberg et al. 2022** (V4, not V1, MRI-guided): "The initial sink following visual stimulation was used as a functional marker to determine the layer 4/5 boundary." — [Westerberg et al. 2022, eLife (PMC8846592)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8846592/)

### Inferences
- The "histologically verified" claim in the sink-alignment literature rests mainly on the Schroeder/Vaughan lesion work, which validated **VEP inversion depths** (N40, N50) and the general 4C location. It did not validate a sink edge to sub-100 µm precision. The anaesthetised histology studies validated verticality and total thickness, not the sink edge itself.
- Total-thickness numbers are mutually consistent:
  - Bijanzadeh: the 4C bottom sits 0.95–1.1 mm below the top of a 1.5–1.7 mm cortex, leaving about 0.5–0.6 mm for L5–6.
  - Dougherty: the bottom of L4 is 1.2 mm from the top and 0.5 mm from the bottom.
  - Zhu: L5/6 is 489 µm.
  - So the sink-based "bottom of L4" lands at roughly 70% of cortical depth in all three. This is a consistency check on the boundary, not a measurement of it.

### Gaps
- I found **no** macaque V1 paper that reports, penetration by penetration, the µm offset between the CSD sink (any landmark) and a histologically drawn 4C/5 or 4Cα/β border.
- I could not access the Xing, Yeh, Burns & Shapley 2012 PNAS full text. A search snippet attributed "sinks in six of eight penetrations" and "lesions in three of eight" to it, but those sentences are verbatim from **Smith et al. 2013** (verified above), so that attribution is wrong. A relative-thickness list (L2/3 0.442, 4A 0.058, 4B 0.108, 4Cα 0.083, 4Cβ 0.083, L5 0.11, L6 0.116) appeared in a snippet with an unclear source. Unverified.
- Self 2013, van Kerkoerle 2014, Maier 2011, Westerberg 2019, Cox 2019, Ninomiya 2015, Nandy 2017, Godlove 2014 and Mitchell 2023 were not readable. Their exact sink rules and any histology are unverified here.

## Q2. Is the initial sink in 4Cα, 4Cβ or all of 4C? Is there an early layer-6 sink? Does the stimulus matter?

### Takeaway
The literature disagrees on the sublayer, and the 4Cα attribution is weaker than its citations suggest. Electrical stimulation puts fast (magno) monosynaptic sinks in 4Cα **and layer 6**, and slow (parvo) sinks in lower 4Cβ and 6 (Mitzdorf & Singer 1979). Flash work from the lesion lineage put the N40 sink in **4Cβ** (Kraut 1985). Schroeder 1998, summarising Givre 1995, describes **separate upper (M) and lower (P) 4C sinks**. Maier 2010 says "possibly with its peak in 4Cα". With 100 µm contacts the early sink usually spans 4C as a whole (~200–400 µm). At 20 µm it fragments into bands about 200 µm thick. Which sublayer dominates depends on the stimulus: black vs white, chromatic, or a small patch vs a full field.

### Cited Findings
- Maier 2010: "this sink in V1 corresponds most closely to layer 4Cα (Mitzdorf and Singer, 1979; Schroeder et al., 1991)". "Previous work … demonstrated that the initial current sink originates in layer 4C, possibly with its peak in layer 4Cα." — [Maier et al. 2010, Front Syst Neurosci (PMC2928665)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2928665/)
- Mitzdorf & Singer 1979 (electrical stimulation): fast afferents gave monosynaptic activity in "layer IV C alpha and layer VI", and slow afferents in "lower layers IV C beta and layer VI". — [PubMed 114553](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/114553/)
- Kraut 1985 (flash): N40 sinks were in "IVCb", and N24 in "IVA". — [PubMed 2408876](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2408876/). This contradicts the 4Cα placement in [Maier et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2928665/).
- Schroeder 1998: "upper and lower Lamina 4C sinks … receive input from M- and P-type thalamic afferents respectively (Givre et al., 1995)". With 150 µm spacing, "responses in the 4C subdivisions are not as well resolved" as at 100 µm. — [Schroeder et al. 1998](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~msereno/569/readings/CSD-visualareas.pdf)
- Zhang 2024 (20 µm spacing):
  - "While there appear to be alternating contacts with a strong short latency sink within L4C, these only span a depth of about 200µm, far narrower than L4C."
  - Black-to-white flips sometimes produce a "zero latency sink" that is "much stronger in layer 4Cβ than 4Cα", attributed to residual signal from the previous transition.
  - White-to-black gave "the most consistent and clear generation of a fast sink corresponding to L4C". White stimuli "can evoke an early L4C source in some penetrations".
  - S-cone stimuli give "relatively large sinks" in 4A and 3B.
  - For the 4Cα/β border they rely on latency: "The electrical signatures that best identify the L4Cα/L4Cβ border are due to the latency differences."
  - [Zhang et al. 2024 (PMC10849622)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)
- Sink extent at 100 µm spacing:
  - Hansen et al. 2012 (anaesthetised, 16-ch at 100 µm, full-field flash, no histology): "the current sink … represents the granular layer and spans ~400 µm" (example, Fig. 1C). — [Hansen et al. 2012, Neuron (PMC3653617)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653617/)
  - Maier 2010: the ±250 µm zone "did match the extent of the initial sink notably well (Figure 2A)". — [PMC2928665](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2928665/)
  - Smith 2013: sinks were "confined to one to two recording sites". — [Smith et al. 2013](https://jiaxx.github.io/assets/img/2012%20Laminar%20dependence%20of%20neuronal%20correlations%20in%20visual%20cortex.pdf)
- Layer 6:
  - van Kerkoerle 2017 (small contour element in the RF): "The earliest neuronal activity occurred in layers 4C and 6". The feedforward MUA response started "in layers 4C and 6 and then spread into the superficial and deep layers". The small element "elicited a relatively weak initial current sink in layer 4C". This is MUA in L6, not a separate L6 sink. — [van Kerkoerle et al. 2017, Nat Commun (PMC5227065)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5227065/)
  - Bijanzadeh 2018: an RF-centred 0.5° square drove the earliest sink in L4C. Far-surround stimuli "evoked earliest CSD signals in lower deep layers – i.e. L5B and 6" and in L1. — [PMC6279245](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279245/)
- Stimulus size:
  - Gieselmann & Thiele 2022 (awake, gratings): the early sink sat at the 4c alignment depth (normalised at 40–60 ms). With large stimuli, "a current sink emerged ~75ms after stimulus onset at –600 µm", where small stimuli produced a source. — [Gieselmann & Thiele 2022, eLife (PMC8916775)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8916775/)
  - Schroeder 1998 compares diffuse flash, pattern/flash and pattern reversal (Fig. 5) in **V2**: the basic sequence is preserved, with more extragranular activation for patterns, as also reported by Schroeder 1991. — [Schroeder et al. 1998](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~msereno/569/readings/CSD-visualareas.pdf); [Schroeder 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1891808/)

### Inferences
- With 100 µm contacts the earliest sink is best read as "4C" (both sublayers, about 200–400 µm). A "4Cα" label is an inference from electrical-stimulation timing (magno is faster). It is not a histological measurement of the visual sink. If the sink is dominated by 4Cβ (P input is denser; Kraut 1985), its **bottom** lies close to the 4C/5 border. If it is dominated by 4Cα, its bottom lies one 4Cβ thickness (~100–150 µm) above the border.
- Grating or small-patch stimuli weight the M/P mix differently from full-field flashes: patterns are more P-driven, and black vs white flashes engage ON/OFF and sustained/transient streams differently. The sink centroid could therefore shift by a sublayer between stimuli. No study measured this shift in µm in V1.
- An early layer-6 component exists on anatomical and electrical-stimulation grounds, and in MUA (van Kerkoerle 2017). In visually evoked CSD at 100 µm it is usually not separable from the 4C sink's return source.

### Gaps
- No study quantifies the M vs P contribution to the visually evoked sink in µm, nor the stimulus-dependent shift of the sink centroid against histology.
- I could not read Givre 1995's laminar figures. The "upper vs lower 4C sinks" claim rests on Schroeder 1998's summary.

## Q3. How far is the 4C/5 boundary below the initial sink (bottom, centre), and with what error?

### Takeaway
**No published macaque V1 study measured this distance against histology.** The numbers in circulation are conventions:
- Maier 2010: L4 = sink centre ±200–250 µm, so the L4 bottom is ~200–250 µm below the sink centre.
- van Kerkoerle 2017: L4 = 50–550 µm above the CSD reversal.
- Maier lab: the 4C/5 border is defined as the sink bottom.

Anatomically, 4C is about 250–300 µm thick (281 µm, Zhu 2026; about 0.17 of cortical depth). The 4C/5 border therefore lies about 125–150 µm below the centre of a sink spanning all of 4C, and about 200–250 µm below the centre of a 4Cα sink. The stated inter-session alignment error is ~200 µm (Maier 2010). Neuropixels data show the CSD reversal can be off by hundreds of µm (Zhang 2024).

### Cited Findings
- Maier 2010:
  - "We treated the center of this sink as a point of alignment … and considered the zone ±200 μm superficial and deep to this reference to be the approximate extent of layer 4."
  - Elsewhere: "±250 μm … intended only as an approximate reference for the upper and lower bounds of layer 4".
  - Across sessions, "data from individual sessions could thus be brought into correspondence, estimated to be within 200 μm (see Figure S5)".
  - The sibling note records the coherence transition "near the bottom of layer 4" at ~1 contact (~100 µm) below the sink centre (Figs. 4B, 5B).
  - [PMC2928665](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2928665/)
- van Kerkoerle 2017 (Roelfsema lab; the same rule as Self 2013 is likely but unverified):
  - "We estimated the location of the border between layer 5 and layer 4C as the polarity reversal from current sinks in layer 4C to current sources in the deep layers around 40 ms after stimulus onset."
  - Compartments: "between 0.55 and 0.05 mm below the 4C/5-boundary were assigned to the deep layers, sites between 0.05 and 0.55 mm above the boundary were assigned to layer 4 and sites between 0.65 and 1.15 mm above the boundary were assigned to the superficial layers", with reference to anatomical thickness studies.
  - CSD used h = 200 µm ("equivalent results with a spacing of 100 μm").
  - [PMC5227065](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5227065/)
  - Their "layer 4" (500 µm) covers all of L4 (4A–4C), not 4C alone. Bijanzadeh 2018 criticised the Self 2013 version of this scheme for "overestimat[ing] the thickness of L4C". — [PMC6279245](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279245/)
- Dougherty 2019 (Maier lab): the border is the sink bottom. "Initial sink" amplitude was taken as the "mean current source density from 0.1 to 0.2 mm" (Fig. 8D), i.e., 100–200 µm above the L4/5 = 0 reference in their depth convention. That places the sink's operational centre ~150 µm above the boundary. This is my reading of the axis convention, not a stated distance. — [PMC6797477](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6797477/)
- Hansen 2012 and Smith 2013 used the sink centroid or strongest-sink site as 0 µm, with no boundary distance. — [PMC3653617](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653617/); [Smith 2013](https://jiaxx.github.io/assets/img/2012%20Laminar%20dependence%20of%20neuronal%20correlations%20in%20visual%20cortex.pdf)
- Gieselmann & Thiele 2022 aligned to "input layer 4c at 0 µm" and used ±450 µm as SG/IG sample depths. — [PMC8916775](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8916775/)
- Histological 4C thickness in the same studies:
  - Zhu 2026: 4C = 281 µm, L5/6 = 489 µm. — [PMC12774416](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12774416/)
  - Smith 2013 cites 300–400 µm for 4C. — [Smith 2013](https://jiaxx.github.io/assets/img/2012%20Laminar%20dependence%20of%20neuronal%20correlations%20in%20visual%20cortex.pdf)
  - Zhang 2024: "the thickness of L4Cα is invariably at least as large as for L4Cβ". — [PMC10849622](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)
- Accuracy of the reversal rule: Zhang 2024 (Fig. 2E) found the old criterion "would mark the bottom of L4C at 1800µm … highly unlikely because there would be insufficient space to accommodate the known thickness of L5 and L6". "The observed transitions do not consistently match to layer identification using the methods that we establish." — [PMC10849622](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)
- Cross-area context (sibling note): Mendoza-Halliday et al. 2024 report a CSD-derived "drop-off" distance of 125 µm from histological L4 (Extended Data Fig. 9, pooled across areas, not V1-specific). Their rebuttal calls the first CSD sink as ground truth "circular" and "less accurate" than histology. — see `lfp_markers.md`; [Mendoza-Halliday et al. 2024 (PMC10917659)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10917659/)

### Inferences
- Simple geometry gives a working prior: with 4C ≈ 280 µm and 4Cα ≈ 4Cβ ≈ 140 µm:
  - from the centre of a whole-4C sink: ≈ 140 µm to the 4C/5 border;
  - from the centre of a 4Cα sink: ≈ 210 µm;
  - from the sink bottom, if the sink fills 4C: ≈ 0 µm;
  - from the sink bottom, if it is 4Cα only: ≈ 140 µm.
  - These are inferences from thickness values and conventions, not measurements. The Maier ±200–250 µm convention is close to the 4Cα-centre case.
- The realistic error on any single 100 µm-spaced estimate is at least ±1 contact (±100 µm), plus smoothing spread (Q5). Maier's 200 µm cross-session figure and Zhu's ±1-group adjustment are the only stated errors.
- Because the anatomical L5+L6 thickness is ~450–600 µm, a sink-derived 4C/5 border can be sanity-checked against the white-matter border, as Zhang did. If the L4/5 border lies less than ~400 µm above the WM transition, it is probably too deep.

### Gaps
- No sink-to-histological-boundary distance with an error estimate exists for macaque V1 in anything I could read.
- I could not read Maier's Fig. S5 (alignment error) or Zhu's channel-group size, so the µm size of "±1 group channel" is unknown.

## Q4. How labs operationally define the L4/5 boundary from CSD, and how accurate that has proved

### Takeaway
There are three conventions:
- **Sink bottom / sink-to-source reversal** = 4C/5 border: Bijanzadeh 2018, van Kerkoerle 2017, Zhu 2026, Dougherty/Westerberg (Maier lab), Hansen 2012 ("base of layer 4").
- **Sink centre** = depth zero, with L4 as ±200–250 µm: Maier 2010, Hansen 2012, Smith 2013, Gieselmann & Thiele 2022.
- **VEP N40 inversion** = base of 4c: Schroeder 1998, from Schroeder 1991.

All trace their validity to the Schroeder/Vaughan lesion work. The only high-resolution test (Zhang 2024, without histology) found the reversal rule unreliable. Mendoza-Halliday 2024 found the early sink identifiable in only 51% of probes across areas, with identifiability falling with probe angle.

### Cited Findings
- Bijanzadeh 2018: "earliest current sink followed by a reversal to current source", citing Schroeder 1998. — [PMC6279245](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279245/)
- Zhu 2026: "the lower boundary of the major sink (the reversal point of sink and source) as the border between layer 4C and layer 5/6", adjusted ±1 group channel by histology. — [PMC12774416](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12774416/)
- van Kerkoerle 2017: the polarity reversal "around 40 ms", citing refs 35 and 73 (by context, Schroeder 1998 / Self 2013; not checked). — [PMC5227065](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5227065/)
- Dougherty 2019: "The bottom of this sink was used as a marker of the transition between granular Layer 4C and the deeper Layer[s]". Westerberg 2022 (V4) uses the "bottom of the granular input sink". — [PMC6797477](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6797477/); [PMC8846592](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8846592/)
- Hansen 2012: "the polarity inversion accompanied by the sink-source configuration at the base of layer 4 (the sink is inside layer 4 …)", citing Maier 2010 and Schroeder 1998, with the sink centre of mass at 0 µm. — [PMC3653617](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653617/)
- Schroeder 1998 used the N40 VEP inversion as the base of 4c. — [Schroeder 1998](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~msereno/569/readings/CSD-visualareas.pdf)
- Zhang 2024: "The criteria from previous studies using the best boundary between an initial sink and a deeper source to mark the border between L4C and L5 is not tenable because sometimes no such transition is apparent and there is never a clearly identifiable transition." Interpolating 100 µm data "so that layer borders could be drawn … at a resolution higher than 100µm" can put the sink-source boundary somewhere that "might deviate substantially from the same boundary in CSD profiles produced by Neuropixels". — [PMC10849622](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)
- Mendoza-Halliday 2024: the CSD early sink was identifiable in 51% of 810 probes (all areas). Image similarity between perpendicular and angled probes was 0.24 for CSD. — [PMC10917659](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10917659/) (via `lfp_markers.md`)

### Inferences
- The "bottom of the sink" and "sink centre ±250 µm" rules put the 4C/5 border at nearly the same depth only if the sink is ~500 µm thick. At 100 µm spacing the sink is ~200–400 µm thick, so the two rules can differ by ~100–150 µm for the same profile.
- The N40-inversion rule (Schroeder) and the sink-bottom rule are not the same landmark either. A potential inversion lies between a sink and its source, so it should sit close to the sink-source reversal. No paper quantifies the difference.

### Gaps
- I found no study that tested sink-bottom against sink-centre rules on the same data with histology.

## Q5. Biases in CSD position estimates: contact gain, spatial smoothing, standard vs inverse CSD

### Takeaway
Spatial smoothing and contact spacing are the documented, quantifiable biases. At 20 µm spacing, Gaussian smoothing with σ = 30 µm reproduces 60 µm-spaced CSDs and changes where sink-source borders appear. Inverse CSD changes depth mixing. Contact gain or impedance mismatch is a known theoretical hazard: through the second difference, one mis-scaled channel creates a spurious sink-source-sink triplet. I found no macaque V1 study that quantified it.

### Cited Findings
- Zhang 2024: Gaussian filters with σ = 20, 30 and 40 µm made "CSD profiles … more stable with increasing degrees of smoothing while missing small details". They were "nearly equivalent" to down-sampling to 40–100 µm spacing (Fig. S1). σ = 30 µm ≈ 60 µm spacing. Unsmoothed 20 µm CSDs are "highly fragmented due to more or less locally balanced high resolution sink-source patterns". — [PMC10849622](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)
- Display interpolation and smoothing are common:
  - Bijanzadeh interpolated "every 10 μm". — [PMC6279245](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279245/)
  - Smith 2013 upsampled ×5 with a 3×3 boxcar "for display purposes only". — [Smith 2013](https://jiaxx.github.io/assets/img/2012%20Laminar%20dependence%20of%20neuronal%20correlations%20in%20visual%20cortex.pdf)
  - Dougherty 2019 smoothed with σ = 0.18 mm in depth (Fig. 8). — [PMC6797477](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6797477/)
  - van Kerkoerle 2017 used a 200 µm differentiation step. — [PMC5227065](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5227065/)
- Inverse vs standard CSD: iCSD differs from the standard second difference through its assumptions about source extent and boundaries (Pettersen et al. 2006). Its effect is on depth mixing; the earlier project report found no paper quantifying its effect on sink onset. — [Pettersen et al. 2006](https://nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/publications/journal_articles/3942)
- Noise: Davis et al. 2023 note that CSD "can be susceptible to noise on individual channels resulting in errors in assigning laminar boundaries". Mendoza-Halliday 2024 report that CSD is sensitive to noise and electrode angle. — via `lfp_markers.md`: [Davis et al. 2023](https://elifesciences.org/articles/84512); [PMC10917659](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10917659/)
- A search for quantitative work on gain/impedance artefacts found only general results. Cross-talk spatially smooths CSD and can induce phase shifts, and Holsheimer found that homogeneous and inhomogeneous conductivity assumptions did not change the sink/source layout in CA1. No primate V1 study was found. — search results, not individually verified ([Frontiers Neural Circuits 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fncir.2016.00051/pdf); [Holsheimer, Exp Brain Res](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00248560))

### Inferences
- The effective smoothing kernels in published pipelines range from σ ≈ 30 µm to 180 µm, or a 200 µm differentiation step. Heavier smoothing widens the sink and moves its bottom edge deeper by up to about half the kernel width, while leaving its centroid in place. The sink centroid is therefore the more processing-robust landmark, and the sink bottom the more processing-sensitive one. This follows from convolution geometry and is not a published measurement.
- A gain mismatch of g on contact k adds roughly (g−1)·V_k·(−2) at k and +(g−1)·V_k at k±1 to the second difference. A 10% gain error on a large-LFP contact can therefore create or shift a sink edge by one contact. Gain-normalising channels, or checking sink-source balance, is the standard defence. This is an inference from the formula, not a cited result.

### Gaps
- No macaque V1 paper quantifies the effect of gain mismatch, smoothing kernel, or iCSD vs standard CSD on the **depth** of the sink bottom against histology.
