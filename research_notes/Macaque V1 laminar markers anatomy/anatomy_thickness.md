# Macaque V1 laminar anatomy: layer depths, thicknesses and geniculate terminations (µm)

Scope: macaque (rhesus / cynomolgus) V1. Every number has its source, species, preparation and shrinkage status where the source gives them. **"Unverified" means I could not read the number in the primary text** (paywall, bot check or figure-only). Several classic tables (O'Kusky & Colonnier 1982, Lund 1973, Hubel & Wiesel 1972, Hendrickson et al. 1978, Fitzpatrick et al. 1985, Callaway 1998) could not be opened in this session, so they are listed under Gaps rather than quoted from memory.

Access note: PMC pages sometimes returned a bot-check page and jneurosci.org PDFs returned 403. Where full text was reachable (NCBI E-utilities or the PMC HTML), numbers were taken from it. Otherwise only abstracts were used, and this is flagged.

## Q1. Total cortical thickness (pia to white matter): fixed vs in vivo, and by eccentricity

### Takeaway
Fixed-tissue macaque V1 is about **1.6–1.7 mm thick in peripheral (calcarine) cortex and about 2.2–2.3 mm in central/parafoveal opercular cortex** (Ahmed et al. 2026, Nissl/NeuN, apparently not shrinkage-corrected). In vivo electrophysiological estimates are **about 1.5–2.0 mm** (Zhang et al. 2024, anesthetized, coverslip-compressed). Depth-axis shrinkage in processed sections was **about 11% (in-plane, pia to WM)** in parafoveal (2–5°) cynomolgus V1 (Kelly & Hawken 2017). For a parafoveal awake rhesus penetration, a working prior of **about 1.7–2.0 mm** (pia to WM) is reasonable, thinning toward the V1/V2 border.

### Cited Findings
- **Ahmed, Duque, Rakic & Molnár 2026, J Anat (doi 10.1111/joa.70137), Table 1** (macaque, 50 µm coronal sections, Nissl and NeuN, QuPath; mean ± SEM). Cortical thickness: opercular (central/foveal/parafoveal) **2260.8 ± 87 µm (Nissl), 2282.3 ± 80.2 µm (NeuN)**; calcarine (peripheral) **1653.3 ± 44.8 µm (Nissl), 1634.9 ± 40.4 µm (NeuN)**. Text values: opercular 2271.6 ± 58.8 vs calcarine 1644.1 ± 29.9 µm. Opercular samples were taken about 4–5 mm from the occipital pole and calcarine samples about 10–14 mm along the calcarine fissure; no eccentricities in degrees are given. **No fixation or shrinkage correction is described.** The animals were 9 macaques (ages E143 to 8 years) including one enucleate, so not all are adults. — [Ahmed et al. 2026, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13399498/)
- The same paper: enucleation reduced cortical thickness to 47.8% of normal posteriorly and 73.1% in calcarine cortex. This is relevant only as evidence that thickness depends on input. — [Ahmed et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13399498/)
- **Zhang, Li & Callaway 2024 (bioRxiv 2024.01.23.576944 v3; eLife reviewed preprint 97290)**: in 31 Neuropixels penetrations in 2 adult male rhesus, overall cortical thickness in vivo was "somewhat more variable than is seen in postmortem histology, ranging from about 1.5–2.0 mm". They attribute the variation to "expansion due to brain swelling or shrinkage due to the placement of a coverslip onto the surface of the cortex in order to generate pressure", which "can vary over the 4–5 days". These influences "are generally expected to generate proportional changes in laminar thickness across the cortical depth". "Cortical thickness is known to decrease closer to the V1/V2 border and this is also seen in our data set (Figure S7A)." Preparation: anesthetized; penetrations excluded for "prolonged duration of maintenance under anesthesia" and for being "very close to the V1/V2 border, where the whole thickness of cortex is thinner". — [Zhang et al. 2024, PMC10849622](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)
- **Kelly & Hawken 2017, Front Neuroanat ("Quantification of neuronal density across cortical depth using automated 3D analysis of confocal image stacks")**: opercular V1 at **eccentricity 2–5°**, 3 cynomolgus monkeys, 50 µm parasagittal vibratome sections. Shrinkage measured with the same blood vessels in processed vs unprocessed adjacent sections: **"mean shrinkage in x was 19 percent, while the mean shrinkage in y was 11 percent"** (y = the pia to white-matter axis in their image strips). Section z-thickness went from 50 µm to a measured ~35 µm (about 29–30% z-shrinkage). V2 z-shrinkage was greater (43%). The paper also cites older studies that "corrected for a linear shrinkage factor of approximately 15%", and one stereology study with "54% tissue shrinkage ... in cortex as a whole". — [Kelly & Hawken 2017, PMC5572544](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5572544/)
- **Sincich, Adams & Horton 2003, Vis Neurosci (flatmounting)**: areal shrinkage from fixation was estimated at "probably under 5%" but called uncertain. This is areal shrinkage in flatmounts, not depth. — [Sincich et al. 2003 PDF](https://www.uab.edu/vsrc/images/Labs/SincichLab/Sincich.Adams.Horton.2003.VN.pdf)
- Sincich et al. (J Neurosci 2010, 30:6963) note that fixed, flatmounted V1 measures about 1.0–1.1 mm, less than in vivo. Flattening compresses tissue, so flatmount thicknesses must not be used as layer-depth priors. (Search-summary level; not re-read in full.) — [J Neurosci 2010](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/30/20/6963)
- **O'Kusky & Colonnier 1982, J Comp Neurol 210:278–290** (adult macaque area 17, stereology, 13 sublaminae): 276 × 10⁶ synapses/mm³ and ~120 × 10³ neurons/mm³ across total thickness. A secondary source gives about 478 × 10⁶ synapses under 1 mm² of surface (40% in layers I–III, 35% in IV, 25% in V–VI). **Unverified: the per-mm² figure and the thickness table were not read in the primary text.** — [Zendy record](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/cne.902100307); [BioNumbers 112217](https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=112217)
- In vivo MRI: an ISMRM 2022 abstract on macaque laminar MRI used a whole-cortex median thickness of 1.9 mm. **No V1-specific in vivo MRI thickness was found.** — [ISMRM 2022 #0424](https://cds.ismrm.org/protected/22MProceedings/PDFfiles/0424.html)

### Inferences
- Implied total thickness from O'Kusky & Colonnier: 478 × 10⁶ synapses/mm² ÷ 276 × 10⁶ synapses/mm³ ≈ **1.73 mm**. This is my arithmetic, uses an unverified per-area figure, and the paper's shrinkage correction is unknown.
- Kelly & Hawken's sampling region (opercular V1, 2–5°) matches this project's recording eccentricity. Their ~11% linear shrinkage along the depth axis means fixed depths should be multiplied by about **1/0.89 ≈ 1.12** to estimate in vivo depths, if the measurements were not already corrected. This applies to their protocol; perfusion and fixation protocols differ.
- Ahmed et al.'s opercular thickness (~2.26 mm) is larger than most physiology estimates (1.5–2.0 mm). Possible reasons: (a) sampling very near the pole (foveal) rather than 2–5°; (b) young animals in the sample; (c) oblique sectioning inflates apparent thickness (coronal sections are not orthogonal to the curved opercular surface everywhere); (d) in vivo compression in the physiology. Treat ~2.2 mm as an upper bound for parafoveal cortex.
- Calcarine (peripheral) cortex is about 27% thinner than opercular cortex in Ahmed et al. (1644/2272). Layer 5 shrinks more than the whole (−40%), so **laminar proportions are not constant across eccentricity**.

### Gaps
- No primary source found that gives V1 thickness as a function of eccentricity in degrees (only opercular vs calcarine).
- No in vivo MRI V1-specific thickness for macaque was found.
- The primary O'Kusky & Colonnier 1982 thickness table (total and per sublamina) could not be read. Values commonly quoted from it (e.g., total ≈1.7–1.8 mm) are therefore unverified here.

## Q2. Thickness and depth-from-pia of each layer (1, 2/3, 4A, 4B, 4Cα, 4Cβ, 5, 6, 6A/6B)

### Takeaway
The only full set of compartment thicknesses I could verify is physiological: **L2/3 ≈ 650 µm, L4A/B ≈ 311 µm, L4C ≈ 281 µm, L5/6 ≈ 489 µm** (Zhu et al. 2024/2026, anesthetized rhesus, 4–10° eccentricity, CSD plus histology). Histology gives **L5 ≈ 315 µm (opercular) and ≈ 190 µm (calcarine)**, with a thin **5A of about 50–100 µm** directly under 4Cβ (Ahmed et al. 2026). **L4A is about 50–100 µm. L1 is about 120–150 µm** (Zhang et al. 2024). No verified source gave separate µm values for 4Cα and 4Cβ. Anatomically, 4Cα is "invariably at least as large" as 4Cβ.

### Cited Findings
- **Zhu, Xia, Chen & Moore 2024, Front Neural Circuits 18:1399571**: "(Mean thickness: 650μm, 311μm, 281μm, 489μm, respectively)" for L2/3, L4A/B, L4C and L5/6. Two adult male rhesus, anesthetized, RF eccentricities ~4–6° (M1) and ~6–10° (M2), Neuropixels. Layer assignment: "located the lower boundary of the major sink (the reversal point of sink and source) as the border between layer 4C and layer 5/6". "Subsequent layer boundaries were determined by offsetting the cortical thickness from histological images", with ±1 channel-group adjustments. — [Zhu et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neural-circuits/articles/10.3389/fncir.2024.1399571/full)
- **Zhu, Oh, Trepka, Chen & Moore 2026, eLife 13:RP103255** repeats the same means (5/6 = 489, 4C = 281, 4A/B = 311, 2/3 = 650 µm), with the same animals and method. Penetrations were opercular (M1) or calcarine (M2), "at angles nearly perpendicular to the cortical surface"; shrinkage was not discussed. One search summary said the 281 µm figure was attributed to O'Kusky & Colonnier and Lund. That conflicts with the methods text, which derives the numbers from the authors' own CSD and histology, so the attribution is **unresolved**. — [Zhu et al. 2026, PMC12774416](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12774416/)
- **Zhang, Li & Callaway 2024** (anesthetized rhesus; their layer template):
  - L4A: "only 50–100 microns thick". When L4A could not be identified: "L4B can be estimated to be half the thickness of L4C, and L4A can be estimated to be 50µm thick".
  - L1/pia: "we simply assign the L1/pia border to a position ~120–150µm above the L1/L2 border, as this is typical in postmortem histology".
  - 4Cα vs 4Cβ: "Anatomical studies reveal a clear change in neuronal density at the L4Cα/L4Cβ border and the thickness of L4Cα is invariably at least as large as for L4Cβ". The border "is always placed such that L4Cβ is not thicker than L4Cα".
  - L2/3A/3B border: about two-thirds of the way from L1/L2 to the top of L4A. **L6A/L6B border: about halfway between the L5/L6A and L6/WM borders.** (These two proportional rules come from the WebFetch summary of the text.)
  - The numeric template thicknesses are only in Fig. 9B, which is an image. **Unverified.**
  - [Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)
- **Ahmed et al. 2026, J Anat, Table 1** (mean ± SEM, µm):

  | Measure | Opercular Nissl | Opercular NeuN | Calcarine Nissl | Calcarine NeuN |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Cortex | 2260.8 ± 87 | 2282.3 ± 80.2 | 1653.3 ± 44.8 | 1634.9 ± 40.4 |
  | Layer 5 | 315.1 ± 11.6 | 318.8 ± 10.2 | 193.8 ± 9.8 | 188.3 ± 9.4 |
  | 5Aα | 62.6 ± 3.2 | 73.7 ± 2.9 | 54.1 ± 3.4 | 52.4 ± 4.8 |
  | 5Aβ | 104.8 ± 5.0 | 114.0 ± 4.4 | 86.1 ± 4.6 | 78.9 ± 4.5 |

  Column assignment is the fetch tool's reading of an ambiguous table layout. Text means: L5 opercular 316.9 ± 7.7 vs calcarine 191.1 ± 7.6 µm; 5Aα overall 68.0 ± 2.25 µm (N = 8, 77 locations). Introduction: layer 5A is "around 50 to 100 μm", citing Lund. The paper **reports no 4C, 4Cβ or layer 6 thicknesses**. — [Ahmed et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13399498/)
- **Neuron numbers per layer** for the whole of V1 (Vanni et al. 2020, Cereb Cortex 30:3483, Table 2, compiled from O'Kusky & Colonnier 1982b and others): 4A 17, 4B 17, **4Cα 14, 4Cβ 24, 5 20, 6 24** × 10⁶ neurons. Total 161 × 10⁶. — [Vanni et al. 2020, PMC7233004](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7233004/)
- Neuronal densities from O'Kusky & Colonnier 1982 (abstract level): **4Cβ 211 × 10³/mm³, 4Cα 137 × 10³/mm³**, mean 120 × 10³/mm³. — [Zendy record](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/cne.902100307)
- Kelly & Hawken 2017 (2–5°, cynomolgus): neuron density depth profiles show "a peak in density corresponding to the upper part of layer 6, as well as a more prominent peak corresponding to layer 4Cβ", and "the gradual increase in density across layer 4C". The laminar boundaries are given as relative depth in figures only. — [Kelly & Hawken 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5572544/)

### Inferences
- **Relative 4Cα vs 4Cβ thickness from volume** (my arithmetic, assuming O'Kusky & Colonnier's numbers and densities are internally consistent): volume ∝ N/density, so 4Cα 14/137 = 0.102 and 4Cβ 24/211 = 0.114. **4Cα and 4Cβ would then be roughly equal in thickness (within ~10%, 4Cβ slightly thicker).** This is in mild tension with Zhang et al.'s rule that 4Cα ≥ 4Cβ. With L4C ≈ 281 µm, each sublayer would be about **130–150 µm**.
- An approximate cumulative depth profile for a ~1.75 mm parafoveal column, built from Zhu's compartments plus Zhang's L1 rule. This is an inference and should be treated as a prior with ±15–20% uncertainty per boundary:

  | Boundary | Depth from pia (µm) | Basis |
  |---|---|---|
  | L1/L2 | ~120–150 | Zhang 2024 rule |
  | top of 4A | ~650 (if Zhu's "2/3" includes L1) to ~800 (if not) | Zhu 2024 |
  | top of 4C | +311 → ~960–1110 | Zhu 2024 |
  | 4Cα/4Cβ | top of 4C + ~140 | volume inference |
  | 4C/5 | top of 4C + 281 → ~1240–1390 | Zhu 2024 (CSD-defined, see caveat) |
  | L6/WM | 4C/5 + 489 → ~1730–1880 | Zhu 2024 |

  The Zhu et al. text does not say whether L1 is folded into "2/3".
- Deep-layer split: Ahmed's L5 fraction of total thickness is 315/2261 = 13.9% (opercular) and 191/1644 = 11.6% (calcarine). Applied to a ~1.75 mm column, that gives **L5 ≈ 200–245 µm**. Then L6 ≈ 489 − (200–245) ≈ **245–290 µm** if Zhu's 489 µm L5+L6 is right. If Zhang et al.'s critique holds (CSD-based 4C bottom placed too deep, see Q3), L5+L6 is larger than 489 µm.

### Gaps
- **No verified primary µm values for 4Cα and 4Cβ separately**, nor for L6 alone or 6A/6B. Lund 1973 (J Comp Neurol 147:455), Hubel & Wiesel 1972 (J Comp Neurol 146:421), Blasdel & Fitzpatrick 1984, Hawken & Parker 1984 and O'Kusky & Colonnier 1982 Table 1 likely contain these but could not be read (paywalled or scanned).
- Garcia-Marin, Kelly & Hawken 2019 (Cereb Cortex 29:134–149) likely reports sublayer boundaries for parafoveal V1, but only the abstract was accessible.
- The numbers in Zhang et al. Fig. 9B (template) are image-only.
- Lund 1988 Annu Rev Neurosci 11:253 was not accessible ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3284442/)).

## Q3. Distances to the 4C/5 boundary, and thickness of L5 and L6 to white matter

### Takeaway
With L4C ≈ 281 µm (Zhu et al.), the 4C/5 boundary lies about **280 µm below the top of 4C**, about **140 µm below the middle of 4C**, and about **130–150 µm below the 4Cα/4Cβ border** (inferred, with 4Cβ ≈ half of 4C). Below that, **L5 is about 190–320 µm** (calcarine to opercular, fixed) and **L5+L6 together are about 490 µm or more** (physiological). On a 100 µm-pitch probe, the 4C/5 boundary is therefore about 1.5 contacts below the 4C midpoint and about 5 contacts above white matter. **The bottom of the early CSD sink is not a reliable marker of the 4C/5 boundary.**

### Cited Findings
- Zhu et al. 2024/2026: L4C = 281 µm and L5/6 = 489 µm (mean, anesthetized rhesus, 4–10°). The 4C/5 border is defined as the lower edge of the major early sink. — [Zhu 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neural-circuits/articles/10.3389/fncir.2024.1399571/full); [Zhu 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12774416/)
- Zhang et al. 2024 argue against using the sink bottom:
  - "criteria applied in previous studies would mark the bottom of L4C at 1800µm, where the initial sink transitions to a source ... But this border location is highly unlikely because there would be insufficient space to accommodate the known thickness of L5 and L6 before our unambiguous identification of the L6/white matter border."
  - The 20 µm-resolution early sink "only span[s] a depth of about 200µm, far narrower than L4C".
  - "the location of a sink-source boundary might deviate substantially from the same boundary in CSD profiles produced by Neuropixels". Previous 100 µm-spacing profiles were interpolated.
  - [Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)
- Zhang et al. 2024 also report that unit density shows a peak at L4C and "the trough below that peak is expected from the low neuron density in L5". The L6/WM border is marked by a drop in spike spread, inverted (axonal) spikes and minimal local coherence. These are deep landmarks independent of CSD. — [Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)
- Ahmed et al. 2026: L5 = 315–319 µm (opercular), 188–194 µm (calcarine), fixed tissue. 5Aα (62–74 µm) and 5Aβ (79–114 µm) lie directly under 4Cβ. "In Nissl sections it is difficult to define the boundary between 4Cβ and 5Aβ", which is offered as a reason for the Nissl/NeuN difference. **The histological 4C/5 border is uncertain by roughly the width of 5Aα (~60–70 µm).** — [Ahmed et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13399498/)
- Zhang et al. 2024: the L6A/L6B border lies about halfway between the L5/L6 and L6/WM borders. — [Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)

### Inferences
Distances below are in µm. "Contacts at 100 µm" divides each distance by the 100 µm pitch.

| Distance | Estimate (µm) | Contacts at 100 µm |
|---|---|---|
| top of 4C → 4C/5 | ~280 (range ~240–330 allowing ±15%) | ~2.8 |
| 4Cα/4Cβ border → 4C/5 | ~130–150 (≈ 4Cβ thickness; ≤ half of 4C per Zhang's rule) | ~1.4 |
| middle of 4C → 4C/5 | ~140 | ~1.4 |
| 4C/5 → L5/L6 | ~190–320 (L5; calcarine–opercular, fixed); ~200–245 scaled to a 1.75 mm column | ~2–3 |
| L5/L6 → WM | ~245–290 (inferred residual; likely larger if 489 µm is an underestimate) | ~2.5–3 |
| 4C/5 → WM | ≥ ~490 (Zhu); ~450–600 plausible after shrinkage and eccentricity adjustment | ~5–6 |

- If fixed-tissue numbers (Ahmed) are used, multiply by ~1.12 for in vivo, using Kelly & Hawken's ~11% depth-axis shrinkage. This assumes Ahmed's numbers are uncorrected, which is not stated.
- Because the early sink spans only ~200 µm, roughly the upper ~70% of 4C (Zhang), an early-sink-centred anchor most likely sits in 4Cα or upper 4Cβ, not at mid-4C. That offset (tens of µm to ~100 µm) matters at 100 µm pitch.

### Gaps
- No verified direct histological measurement of "4Cα/β border to 4C/5 border" in µm.
- No verified L6 thickness or 6A/6B thicknesses in µm.

## Q4. Where M, P and K geniculate axons terminate, and layer 6 / layer 5 geniculate input

### Takeaway
**M axons terminate in 4Cα**: the upper-4Cα group sends collaterals to 6B, the lower-two-thirds group has few or none. **P axons terminate in 4Cβ** (small branches into lower 4Cα), with a separate P population to **4A**. **K axons go to layer 1 (with collaterals to 6A) and to the layer 3 blobs.** There is a thalamic input to layer 6, but it is sparse and qualitative in the sources I could read. I found **no evidence of direct geniculate termination in layer 5**. Thalamic synapse density is highest in 4Cβ. Feedforward synapses are ~20% of excitatory synapses in 4Cβ and ~15% in 4Cα.

### Cited Findings
- **Blasdel & Lund 1983, J Neurosci 3:1389–1413** (HRP-filled axons, n = 38, 9 recorded intracellularly; macaque). Verbatim abstract, listing five populations of LGN afferents:
  1. "those to layer 4C beta, which have extremely circumscribed, dense terminal fields (small branches of which occasionally intrude into 4C alpha) but which have not been shown to project to other laminae";
  2. "afferents to layer 4A, which in some cases send fine ascending collaterals into layer 2–3 and which do not, apparently, send collaterals to other laminae";
  3. "afferents to layer 1, which are fine, extend over large distances horizontally, and send collaterals to layer 6A";
  4. "afferents to the lower two-thirds of layer 4C alpha, which have few or no collaterals in layer 6";
  5. "afferents to the upper half of layer 4C alpha, which have arborizing collaterals in layer 6B".

  4Cβ and layer-1 axons were colour-selective (P-like). 4Cα axons were "transient, broad band and highly contrast-sensitive" (M-like). "Afferents to 4C alpha were found to cover approximately 6 times as much surface area as afferents to 4C beta." — [Blasdel & Lund 1983, PMC6564440](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6564440/)
- **Vanni et al. 2020 review (Cereb Cortex 30:3483)**, summarizing Blasdel & Lund 1983 and Freund et al. 1989:
  - **M**: "denser projections to V1 L4Cα" and "sparser and fine axon collaterals to the lower part of L6". Each cluster covers 0.3–0.4 mm² and contains about 6,490 synapses.
  - **P**: most dominant projection to L4Cβ, "with sparser projections to L4A and the upper part of L6". L4Cβ clusters cover 0.067 mm², with about 3,154 synapses per cluster (Freund). An L4A axon has 764 boutons over 0.058 mm².
  - **K1–K2** axons: ~47% of boutons in L1, 38% in L3A, 12% in lower L3, 3% in L2.
  - **K3–K6** axons: ~93% in lower L3 (3Bα, mainly CO blobs), with 2% L1, 3% upper L3 and 2% L4A.
  - [Vanni et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7233004/)
- **Conflict on P → layer 6**: Vanni's "P to upper L6" disagrees with Blasdel & Lund's statement that 4Cβ afferents "have not been shown to project to other laminae". In Blasdel & Lund, the 6A collaterals come from the (K-like) layer-1 afferents. The P→upper-6 claim probably derives from bulk-label studies (Hendrickson, Wilson & Ogren 1978), which I could not verify.
- **Freund, Martin, Soltesz, Somogyi & Whitteridge 1989, J Comp Neurol 289:315–336** (2 M and 2 P physiologically identified axons, EM): "few collaterals formed varicosities in layer 6" (abstract-level, via aggregator). Synapses per bouton: M 2.1, P 1.79 and 2.6. Aggregator snippets disagree on whether the M axons arborized mainly in 4Cα or 4Cβ; 4Cα is the conventional reading. — [Scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/3a3a7d46eba9ec61987780d5e78398cc); [Zendy record](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/cne.902890211)
- **Garcia-Marin, Kelly & Hawken 2019, Cereb Cortex 29:134–149** (online 2017; doi 10.1093/cercor/bhx311). VGluT2 thalamic boutons, 3D EM plus confocal, trichromatic primates. Abstract: "the average feedforward contribution was about 20% of the total excitatory input in the parvocellular (P) pathway, about 3 times the currently accepted values ... In the magnocellular (M) pathway it was around 15%". "Total synaptic and cell densities were as much as 150% of currently accepted values." A secondary summary says thalamocortical synapse density was highest in 4Cβ, with a dense VGluT2 band in upper 4C (4Cα). **Layer 6 VGluT2 densities were not accessible.** — [Garcia-Marin et al. 2019, PMC6490972](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6490972/)
- Ahmed et al. 2026 restate 4Cα = M and 4Cβ = P, and say 4B receives sparse or no LGN input. They make no claim about LGN input to layer 5. — [Ahmed et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13399498/)
- Layer 6 → LGN feedback (opposite direction, context only): L6A and L6B project to the P and M LGN layers respectively (Briggs & Usrey 2009, cited in Vanni et al. 2020). — [Vanni et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7233004/)

### Inferences
- Layer 6 geniculate input exists but is a small fraction of 4C input: "sparse/fine collaterals" (Vanni), "few" (Freund), "few or no" for lower-4Cα axons (Blasdel & Lund). A short-latency deep sink or early spikes in 6B could reflect direct M collaterals. Upper-6 (6A) input would come from K/layer-1 afferents (per Blasdel & Lund) or possibly P (per Vanni).
- The upper-4Cα M population's collateral to 6B makes 4Cα and lower 6 the two depths with the shortest-latency M input, separated by roughly L4Cβ + L5 + 6A ≈ 500–600 µm (inferred from Q3).
- No source supports direct LGN synapses in layer 5. Early layer 5 activity is more plausibly mediated by 4C or 6 neurons, or by apical dendrites of deep neurons passing through 4C (the last is not sourced here).

### Gaps
- No quantitative LGN terminal density in layer 6 vs 4C (e.g., VGluT2 boutons/mm³ per layer) was accessible. Garcia-Marin et al. 2019 full text is needed.
- Hendry & Yoshioka 1994 (Science) and Hendrickson et al. 1978 could not be read. K→blob projections are cited only through the Vanni review.
- Callaway 1998, Nassi & Callaway 2009 and Sincich & Horton 2005 reviews were not opened.

## Q5. Probe geometry: obliquity, dimpling/compression and shrinkage effects on apparent thickness

### Takeaway
Quantified sources were limited to **shrinkage**: ~11% along the depth axis, 19% across, and ~29% section z-shrinkage in processed parafoveal V1 (Kelly & Hawken 2017). In vivo **compression or swelling** produces penetration-to-penetration total thickness of 1.5–2.0 mm that is "expected to generate proportional changes" across layers (Zhang et al. 2024). **No quantified dimpling data for awake macaque V1 were found.** Obliquity effects follow from geometry (see Inferences).

### Cited Findings
- Kelly & Hawken 2017: shrinkage x 19%, y (depth axis) 11%, section thickness 50 → ~35 µm. V2 shrank more than V1 (z 43%). Other literature applied ~15% linear correction, and one study measured 54% volumetric shrinkage. — [Kelly & Hawken 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5572544/)
- Zhang et al. 2024: a coverslip on the cortex for stability "lightly" compresses tissue. Swelling or compression varies over 4–5 recording days. Movements can be "rigid" shifts or "non-uniform changes ... (such as compression of superficial layers during initial introduction of an electrode)". Probes were inserted perpendicular to the cortex. — [Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)
- Zhang et al. 2024 on contact spacing: 100 µm-pitch arrays cannot "precisely assign laminar boundaries or ... detect thin layers such as L4A, which is less than 100 µm thick". Smoothed CSD sink and source sizes best match layer thicknesses at ~50 µm spacing. — [Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)
- Zhu et al. 2026 used near-perpendicular penetrations, supported by modal orientation-preference differences across depth of 1–5°. — [Zhu et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12774416/)
- Ahmed et al. 2026: thickness differs between Nissl and NeuN at the 4Cβ/5A border by ~10 µm (5Aα 62.6 vs 73.7 µm). Stain choice alone moves the histological border. — [Ahmed et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13399498/)

### Inferences
- **Obliquity** (geometry, my arithmetic): apparent thickness along the probe = true thickness / cos θ. Inflation is +1.5% at 10°, +6.4% at 20°, +15.5% at 30° and +30.5% at 40°. A 281 µm 4C would appear as 299 µm at 20° and 325 µm at 30°. On the opercular surface near the pole, the cortex curves, so the angle can change along a 3.2 mm (32 × 100 µm) shank.
- **Uniform compression** (coverslip, dura, dimpling) scales all layers proportionally, per Zhang et al.'s expectation. Total pia-to-WM length on the probe can then be used to rescale a template; a 1.5 vs 2.0 mm column implies about ±14% scaling around a 1.75 mm mean.
- **Non-uniform superficial compression** during insertion, which Zhang et al. raise, would shrink apparent L1–3 more than deep layers. Anchoring deep distances on 4C or on the L6/WM transition is more robust than anchoring on pia.
- **Fixed-to-in-vivo conversion**: multiply fixed depths by ~1.12 (11% shrinkage) for Kelly & Hawken-type processing. With unknown protocols, a 1.10–1.20 range is a defensible prior. The ~15% linear correction used in older literature sits within this range.
- **100 µm contact pitch**: 4Cβ (~130–150 µm) spans 1–2 contacts, L4A (~50–100 µm) at most one, and 5Aα (~60–70 µm) less than one. Boundary localization of ±50 µm is the sampling floor before any anatomical uncertainty.

### Gaps
- No quantitative measurement of dimpling or compression of awake macaque V1 under a linear array (e.g., µm of surface depression vs insertion depth) was found.
- No direct in vivo vs post-mortem comparison of the same V1 penetrations, giving a shrinkage factor for electrolytic-lesion reconstructions, was found in accessible text.
