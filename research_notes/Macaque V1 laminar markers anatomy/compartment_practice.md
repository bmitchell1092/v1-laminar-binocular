# Laminar compartment practice in macaque V1 linear-array studies: depth reference, compartment rules, and alignment error

Scope: published laminar recordings (mostly ~100 µm contact spacing; some Neuropixels at 20 µm) in macaque V1, plus a few V4/other-area papers whose methods bear on alignment error. Verbatim quotes come from full texts pulled from Europe PMC or bioRxiv, except where marked "[summary]". Those items came through a page-summarising fetch tool rather than full text I read myself, so check the wording before quoting it. "PR" = peer-reviewed, "PP" = preprint.

## 1. Which reference landmark and which compartment rules do labs use?

### Takeaway
Most labs anchor at the **bottom of the earliest (initial) current sink**, taken as the L4C/L5 border. They then cut fixed-width compartments of about 0.5 mm: granular 0 to +0.5 mm, supragranular above that, infragranular 0 to −0.5 mm. There are real variants. Maier 2010 and Hansen 2012 anchor at the **centre** of the sink (±200–250 µm). Ferro 2021 uses a ±0.25 mm granular band. Drebitz 2024 anchors at the **top** of the sink (the L3/L4 border). Zhu/Moore 2024–2026 offset from the sink bottom by histological layer thicknesses. van Kerkoerle 2017 leaves a 0.1 mm gap between compartments.

### Cited findings

**Maier lab and descendants (awake, ~100 µm spacing)**
- Maier et al. 2010 (Front Syst Neurosci, PR; awake V1). The anchor is the **centre** of the initial sink: "We treated the center of this sink as a point of alignment (the 'zero point') for each session, and considered the zone ±200 μm superficial and deep to this reference to be the approximate extent of layer 4, though the results do not critically depend on this approximation." Elsewhere the same paper says: "The boundaries of these zones, defined as ±250 μm (corresponding to an inclusion criterion of two channels above and below the one upon which we centered the data) are intended only as an approximate reference for the upper and lower bounds of layer 4." Its justification is that "the initial current sink originates in layer 4C, possibly with its peak in layer 4Cα (Mitzdorf and Singer, 1979; Schroeder et al., 1991)". Note the internal ±200 vs ±250 µm inconsistency. — [Maier, Adams, Aura & Leopold 2010](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnsys.2010.00031)
- Dougherty, Cox, Westerberg & Maier 2019 (Curr Biol, PR; bioRxiv v2 read) [summary]. Methods: "CSD analysis was used to locate the boundary between layer 4C and layer 5". Fig. 3 legend: "0 mm marks the L4C/L5 border, estimated using the bottom of the initial current sink". "The supragranular-to-granular boundary is more challenging to define… and was instead set to 0.5 mm above the granular to infragranular boundary". Granular is "0.0 to 0.5 mm relative to L4C/L5 boundary". Contacts were also classified using "the power spectral density of the LFP across cortical depth", "signal correlations of the LFP between all contact combinations" and "stimulus-evoked analog multiunit responses". The stated alignment resolution is "0.1 mm +/− 0.05 mm". Contacts with no visual response at the array ends were excluded, and aMUA was used "to identify electrode contacts that lie outside V1". N = 33 penetrations had a flash CSD and N = 45 had a grating CSD. — [Dougherty et al. 2019, bioRxiv 10.1101/320218v2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/320218v2.full); [Curr Biol version](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2018.12.004)
- Cox, Dougherty, Westerberg, Schall & Maier 2019 (J Vis, PR; awake V1). "The bottom of this sink was used as a marker of the transition between granular Layer 4C and the deeper Layer 5." The compartments are uneven and leave gaps: "upper, supragranular layers (0.6 mm and above), the middle, granular layers (0.1–0.5 mm), and the deep, infragranular layers (−0.1 mm and below)". The paper also gives an anatomical figure: "On average, the bottom of the granular Layer 4 was located 1.2 mm from the top of V1 and 0.5 mm from the bottom of V1." — [Cox et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1167/19.12.13)
- Westerberg, Schall, Woodman & Maier 2023 (Nat Commun, PR; **V4**, not V1). "Sessions were aligned using the bottom of the initial feedforward input sink as a functional marker." "'middle' refers to the estimate of the granular input layer 4 (0.5 mm space above the CSD initial sink functional marker), 'upper' refers to the estimate of supragranular layers 2 and 3 (0.5 mm space above the L4 compartment), and 'lower' refers to the estimate of infragranular layers 5 and 6 (0.5 mm space below the L4 compartment)." A supplementary sink measure used "the average of 5 recording channels immediately above the L4/5 boundary". — [Westerberg et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-41745-1)
- Westerberg et al. 2022 (eLife, PR; V4). "The initial sink following visual stimulation was used as a functional marker to determine the layer 4/5 boundary." Perpendicularity was checked by RF alignment across depth. — [Westerberg, Schall, Maier, Woodman & Schall 2022](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.72139)
- Ninomiya et al. 2015 (J Neurophysiol, PR; Maier co-author) reports the zero channel at the bottom of the initial sink as "typically located 0.8–1.3 [mm] below the dura" (the source text says "μm", an obvious typo). I took this from a search-result snippet and did not re-read it in full text. — [Ninomiya et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4440241/)
- Carlson, Mitchell, Westerberg, Poggi, Woodman & Maier 2025 (bioRxiv, **PP**; awake V1; monkeys "B" and "J", which are probably the same animals as this dataset, unverified) [summary]. Alignment located "the bottom of granular layer IVc" from the initial sink. PSD was used as a check: "PSD validated that we recorded from the first fold of cortex by observing theta and gamma in the upper layers and alpha and beta activity in the lower layers". The text adds that the technique "is most robust when all these metrics are compared concomitantly". Penetrations were kept only if they "spanned at least 0.5 mm (5 laminar microelectrode contacts) within each laminar compartment". That took 44 penetrations to 31 aligned and then 27 analysed (8 B, 19 J). No µm compartment definitions were found in the part I read. — [Carlson et al. 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.04.680461v1.full)
- Mitchell et al. 2023 (Curr Biol, PR): the full text could not be retrieved (Cell returned 403; Europe PMC returned a 500 error). Its rule is presumably the Dougherty 2019 rule, but I did not verify that. See Gaps. — [Mitchell, Carlson, Westerberg, Cox & Maier 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2023.08.019)

**Roelfsema lab (awake V1)**
- van Kerkoerle, Self & Roelfsema 2017 (Nat Commun, PR). "We estimated the location of the border between layer 5 and layer 4C as the polarity reversal from current sinks in layer 4C to current sources in the deep layers around 40 ms after stimulus onset." "Recording sites between 0.55 and 0.05 mm below the 4C/5-boundary were assigned to the deep layers, sites between 0.05 and 0.55 mm above the boundary were assigned to layer 4 and sites between 0.65 and 1.15 mm above the boundary were assigned to the superficial layers." The rule therefore drops a ±0.05 mm band at the boundary and a 0.55–0.65 mm gap. On angle: "When the RFs of the recording sites in different layers did not overlap, indicating that the probe was not perpendicular to the cortical surface, the probe was retracted and inserted at a different location." — [van Kerkoerle et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms13804)
- Self et al. 2013 (Curr Biol) and van Kerkoerle et al. 2014 (PNAS): I could not get the full texts (403 and 500 errors). Davis et al. 2023 cite Self 2013 for the convention that "the current sources above and below are used to estimate the boundaries with respect to superficial (layers 1–3) and deep (layers 5 and 6) cortical layers". — [Davis et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.84512)

**Thiele lab (awake V1)**
- Ferro, van Kempen, Boyd, Panzeri & Thiele 2021 (PNAS, PR). The reference comes from "the earliest current sink across laminae using CSD of LFPs and by analyzing the shortest latency of the stimulus-evoked MUA E response". Then: "For V1, channels above the reference channel at distances of 0.25 to 1 mm were labeled as supragranular, channels above or below the reference channel within 0.25 mm were labeled as granular, and channels below reference at distance range 0.25 to 0.75 mm were labeled as infragranular." For V4 the granular band was ±0.1 mm. From the text I read, the reference is centred on the sink rather than on its bottom; this is unverified. — [Ferro et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2022097118)

**Kreiter lab (awake V1): top-of-sink anchor**
- Drebitz, Rausch, Domingo Gil & Kreiter 2024 (Front Neural Circuits, PR). The authors "identified the border between layer III and layer IV based on the inversion of the CSD's polarity from the source closest to the cortical surface to the initial sink below". Then "Recording sites located within 0.5 mm below this border were categorized as granular, and sites further below as infragranular (Maier et al., 2010; Van Kerkoerle et al., 2014; Ferro et al., 2021)." — [Drebitz et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncir.2024.1490638)

**Dragoi lab (awake V1): sink centroid**
- Hansen, Chelaru & Dragoi 2012 (Neuron, PR). "The contact with the sink centroid served as granular layer reference at 0 μm". Contacts above and below were grouped as SG, G or IG (exact widths are in the supplement, not read). "In the example the current sink … represents the granular layer and spans ~400 μm." — [Hansen et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2012.08.029)

**Angelucci lab (anaesthetised macaque and marmoset V1)**
- Bijanzadeh, Nurminen, Merlin, Clark & Angelucci 2018 (Neuron, PR; anaesthetised macaque). "Input L4C can be identified as the location of the earliest current sink followed by a reversal to current source". "The boundaries between other layers were estimated based on previous anatomical studies (Lund, 1973) and postmortem histology, therefore, these boundaries are necessarily tentative." "Upper-L4" runs "from the uppermost part of L4C to approximately 1–2 contacts above the top of 4C". The study used "10 penetrations deemed to be perpendicular", with verticality "verified in vivo by the alignment of RF location and similarity of orientation tuning functions across layers … and confirmed by postmortem histology". — [Bijanzadeh et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.08.020)
- Nurminen et al. 2018 (Nat Commun, PR; anaesthetised **marmoset**). "The earliest current sink corresponds to the granular layer, and its borders with the supra- and infra-granular layers can be determined from the reversals from current sink to current source above and below the granular layer." — [Nurminen et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-04500-5)

**Moore lab (awake V1, Neuropixels, CSD plus histology)**
- Zhu, Xia, Chen & Moore 2024 (Front Neural Circuits, PR) and Zhu et al. 2026 (eLife, PR). "We located the lower boundary of the major sink (the reversal point of sink and source) as the border between layer 4C and layer 5/6. Subsequent layer boundaries were determined by offsetting the cortical thickness from histological images. For example, the boundary between layers 4A/B and layer 4C was determined by offsetting the thickness of layer 4C in the histology image." The compartments are "layers 2/3, 4A/B, 4C, and 5/6 (Mean thickness: 650 μm, 311 μm, 281 μm, 489 μm, respectively)". Probes went "into the lateral operculum of macaque V1 … at angles nearly perpendicular to the cortical surface". — [Zhu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncir.2024.1399571); [Zhu et al. 2026 eLife](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.103255)
- Carr, Zhu, …, Moore & Chandrasekaran 2025 (bioRxiv, PP) used DiI tracks plus CSD, "scaled to average layer boundaries across the five sessions"; the span of 4A/B plus 4C was "0 µm – 588.8 µm" [summary]. — [Carr et al. 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.05.14.653875v1.full)

**Reynolds lab (V4 macaque and marmoset MT/PFC; not V1)**
- Davis, Dotson, Franken, Muller & Reynolds 2023 (eLife, PR). "We took from the resulting evoked source–sink patterns the earliest sink (red) as the location of the input layers and assigned our reference depth relative to the input layer as the bottom of the current sink, with the boundaries to the sources (blue) above and below identifying the boundaries of the superficial and deep cortical layers." The authors call this "standard … (Franken and Reynolds, 2021; Nandy et al., 2017; Schroeder et al., 1998; Self et al., 2013; Westerberg et al., 2022)". The spike-phase (LPC) boundary was "selected by eye". — [Davis et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.84512)

**Bastos/Miller spectrolaminar alignment**
- Mendoza-Halliday et al. 2024 (Nat Neurosci, PR) aligned probes by the alpha-beta/gamma crossover channel instead of the sink: "Laminar depths are measured with respect to the alpha-beta/gamma crossover." The FLIP algorithm uses alpha-beta (10–19 Hz) and gamma (75–150 Hz) bands and "identifies the alpha-beta peak …, gamma peak … and crossover … channels as markers for layers 5/6, 2/3 and 4, respectively". — [Mendoza-Halliday et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01554-7)

### Inferences
- In awake macaque V1 the dominant convention, used by Maier, Roelfsema, Reynolds and Moore, is a sink-bottom anchor (the L4C/L5 sink-to-source reversal). The exact compartment edges differ by up to about 0.15 mm between labs: Dougherty uses 0–0.5, Cox 0.1–0.5, and van Kerkoerle 0.05–0.55 with a gap. Centre-of-sink anchors (Maier 2010, Hansen 2012, likely Ferro 2021) shift the reference by roughly half a sink width, about 0.1–0.2 mm, relative to sink-bottom anchors.
- The "granular = 0.5 mm above L4/5" rule was adopted by convention, not from anatomy. Dougherty 2019 says outright that the upper boundary "was instead set to 0.5 mm above" because it is hard to define.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the verbatim rules for Mitchell et al. 2023 (Curr Biol), Self et al. 2013, van Kerkoerle et al. 2014, Xing et al. 2012 (PNAS; lesion-histology study), Maier et al. 2011 (J Neurosci), Westerberg et al. 2019 (J Neurophysiol), Nandy et al. 2017 (V4) or Schroeder et al. 1998. Publisher pages returned 403 and Europe PMC returned 500 errors or only headers. The Maier 2011 snippet says the infragranular LFP response was "centered ∼500 μm below the initial layer 4C current sink", but that is from a search snippet only ([J Neurosci](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/31/6/1971)).
- Trautmann et al. 2025 (Nat Neurosci, Neuropixels NHP) contains no V1 laminar-compartment rule that I could find.
- I did not establish Hansen 2012's SG/IG widths (supplement) or confirm whether Ferro 2021 places its reference at the centre or the bottom of the sink.

## 2. Compartment widths and their anatomical justification

### Takeaway
Histology-based thicknesses in macaque V1 (Zhu/Moore) are about 650 µm for L2/3, 311 µm for 4A/B, **281 µm for 4C** and **489 µm for L5/6**. So the usual 0.5 mm "granular" window above L4/5 covers all of 4C plus most of 4A/B. The 0.5 mm infragranular window roughly matches L5/6. The 0.5 mm supragranular window covers only part of L2/3. Cox 2019 puts the L4/5 border on average 1.2 mm below the top of V1 and only 0.5 mm above the bottom of V1.

### Cited findings
- Mean thicknesses of 650, 311, 281 and 489 µm (L2/3, 4A/B, 4C, 5/6), "consistent with previous anatomical O'Kusky and Colonnier, 1982; Lund, 1973 and CSD Self et al., 2013; Chen et al., 2017 studies". — [Zhu et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.103255); [Zhu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncir.2024.1399571)
- "On average, the bottom of the granular Layer 4 was located 1.2 mm from the top of V1 and 0.5 mm from the bottom of V1" (awake, Maier lab, 100 µm arrays). — [Cox et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1167/19.12.13)
- Maier 2010 judged that a ±200–250 µm band around the sink centre "did match the extent of the initial sink notably well". — [Maier et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnsys.2010.00031)
- Zhang, Li & Callaway (bioRxiv 2024, PP; anaesthetised; Neuropixels 20 µm): "in macaque primary visual cortex (V1) there are far more layers, such as 4A which is only 50–100 microns thick". With 100 µm spacing "the vertical spacing of electrodes is too large … to precisely assign laminar boundaries or to detect thin layers such as L4A". In 20 µm CSDs, contacts with a strong short-latency sink "only span a depth of about 200µm, far narrower than L4C, leaving considerable uncertainty about where boundaries should be drawn". — [Zhang, Li & Callaway 2024](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.01.23.576944)
- Bijanzadeh 2018: layer boundaries other than L4C, taken from Lund 1973 and histology, "are necessarily tentative". — [Bijanzadeh et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.08.020)

### Inferences
- If 4C is about 0.28 mm and 4A/B about 0.31 mm, then "granular = 0–0.5 mm" mixes 4Cβ, 4Cα and most of 4B. That matters for binocular questions, because 4B and upper 4Cα differ from 4Cβ in ocularity and magno/parvo input. A narrower granular band of about 0.3 mm (Zhu/Moore) or the ±0.25 mm band (Ferro) fits the anatomy better. At 100 µm spacing it holds only about 3 contacts.
- L5/6 is about 0.49 mm (Zhu), and the L4/5 border sits about 0.5 mm above the bottom of V1 (Cox). So **any sink-bottom estimate that leaves much less than about 0.4–0.5 mm of responsive grey matter above the white matter, or sits much more than about 1.2–1.3 mm below the top of cortex, is anatomically suspect.** Callaway's group used exactly this logic to reject a CSD-derived border (see section 4).
- Between labs, the same "infragranular" label covers −0.05 to −0.55 mm, 0 to −0.5 mm, −0.25 to −0.75 mm, or "−0.1 and below". Cross-study comparisons of "deep-layer" effects therefore carry about 0.25 mm of definitional slop before any alignment error.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve primary anatomical thickness tables (Lund 1973; O'Kusky & Colonnier 1982) or eccentricity and shrinkage corrections for parafoveal V1. Zhu's values come from lateral-operculum penetrations and may differ in calcarine cortex.

## 3. Penetrations with no clear sink, disagreeing markers, oblique probes or two cortical sheets

### Takeaway
The usual practice is to **exclude**, not repair. Labs drop penetrations with no discernible CSD sink, non-perpendicular penetrations (judged by RF overlap or orientation consistency across depth), penetrations not covering enough of V1, and penetrations not in the first fold. Few papers give an explicit rule for markers that disagree; the clearest are Davis 2023 (≥200 µm flagged) and Callaway's anatomical-plausibility test. Exclusion rates are large: 51% of probes had an identifiable sink across areas (Mendoza-Halliday), and the Maier-lab V1 study kept 27 of 44 penetrations (Carlson 2025).

### Cited findings
- Westerberg 2023 (V4): "The remaining 41 sessions were found to either not have a discernable CSD profile for laminar alignment, not be orthogonal to the cortical surface, or not have enough priming blocks … and were thus removed from analysis." — [Westerberg et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-41745-1)
- Carlson 2025 (PP, V1) [summary]: "Laminar recordings with only a partial representation of V1 were removed". PSD was used to confirm "the first fold of cortex". A ≥0.5 mm per-compartment coverage criterion took 44 penetrations to 31 aligned and then 27 analysed. — [Carlson et al. 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.04.680461v1.full)
- van Kerkoerle 2017: non-overlapping RFs meant "the probe was retracted and inserted at a different location". Fewer than 5% of channels were interpolated as bad. — [van Kerkoerle et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms13804)
- Bijanzadeh 2018: only penetrations "deemed to be perpendicular" by RF alignment, orientation-tuning similarity and histology were used (10 penetrations, 4 macaques). — [Bijanzadeh et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.08.020)
- Mendoza-Halliday 2024: "An identifiable early CSD sink … was present in 51% of the 810 probes", against 61% (manual), 64% (FLIP) and 81% (vFLIP) with a spectrolaminar crossover. "CSD sink identification was significantly lower for high-angle probes (P = 0.001972)", while crossover identification did not depend on angle. Across probes "the characteristics of the early sink, including duration, intensity and laminar thickness, appeared to vary markedly". Areas were mixed; sinks were more identifiable in V4, MT and MST than in LIP and PFC. — [Mendoza-Halliday et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01554-7)
- Zhang/Li/Callaway 2024 (PP): "The criteria from previous studies using the best boundary between an initial sink and a deeper source to mark the border between L4C and L5 is not tenable because sometimes no such transition is apparent and there is never a clearly identifiable transition." They give a concrete case of a rejected CSD border: "criteria applied in previous studies would mark the bottom of L4C at 1800µm … But this border location is highly unlikely because there would be insufficient space to accommodate the known thickness of L5 and L6 before our unambiguous identification of the L6/white matter border". They also warn that "in some cases the CSD profile in response to a black to white screen has a zero latency sink in L4C", which they attribute to "residual signal from the prior white to black transition", a possible source of a spurious "earliest" sink. 4 of 35 penetrations were excluded (criteria are in their Methods, not read). — [Zhang, Li & Callaway 2024](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.01.23.576944)
- Dougherty 2019: aMUA was used "to identify electrode contacts that lie outside V1", and non-responsive end contacts were excluded [summary]. — [Dougherty et al. 2019](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/320218v2.full)
- Davis 2023: "Across our recordings there were some instances (14/34) where there was 200 μm or more disagreement between CSD and LPC estimates for the location of the input layer". One example "shows the characteristic phase reversal 600 μm below the CSD estimate". For those 14 sessions the authors compared firing-rate depth profiles aligned by each method (Fig. 6a); the outcome is not captured in my extract. — [Davis et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.84512)
- Mackey et al. (bioRxiv 2024, PP; published as Nat Neurosci 2026, doi 10.1038/s41593-025-02167-y, which I did not read) [summary]: in their V1 data FLIP fitted only about 50% of penetrations, and "FLIP produced inverted (negative G) fits in 2 cases each in A1 and V1", which they call anatomically implausible on flat cortex. — [Mackey et al. 2024 preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.18.613490v1.full)

### Inferences
- I found **no published rule for a probe that crosses two cortical sheets**, such as operculum into calcarine or a second fold. The only explicit handling is the Maier lab's requirement (Carlson 2025) that the recording come from the "first fold", checked by the PSD gradient, and that each compartment have ≥0.5 mm of coverage. In practice those sessions are excluded or truncated at the first white-matter gap. A plausible gap signature is low aMUA or LFP power, or an inverted gradient. That is my inference, not a published rule.
- Callaway's anatomical-plausibility test (the L4/5 border must leave about 0.5 mm of L5/6 above white matter), combined with Cox 2019's averages (1.2 mm below the top, 0.5 mm above the bottom), gives a defensible, citable rejection criterion for a misplaced "earliest sink". This project's existing sensitivity check in `docs/09_paper_design.md` (S3: exclude sinks more than 0.5 mm below L4/5 or more than 1.5 mm below the top of cortex) is consistent with that logic.

### Gaps
- None of the papers I read reports how double sinks were resolved. I also found no numeric criterion for "perpendicular", such as a maximum RF shift per mm of depth.

## 4. Quantitative comparisons of alignment methods (CSD sink, spectrolaminar crossover, spike-phase, histology)

### Takeaway
Reported disagreement between markers is typically **0.1–0.3 mm**. Davis 2023 found a mean |CSD − spike-phase| of about 271 µm, with 41% of sessions at ≥200 µm and a worst example of 600 µm. Mendoza-Halliday's crossover sat 27–58 µm from the histological centre of L4 (n = 8 and 10 probes, outside V1), with 95% CIs spanning about ±200 µm. Within-session drift adds 100–300 µm. Validation in V1 specifically is thin: n = 3 histology probes in Mendoza-Halliday, and the Mackey critique reports the crossover in L4 in only 8% of V1 penetrations with FLIP. **A 0.4–1.5 mm disagreement exceeds every reported typical error.**

### Cited findings
- **Davis 2023 (CSD vs spike-phase, V4 and marmoset MT/PFC, N = 34).** "There was a significant correlation between the depth estimated from the CSD and the phase reversal (r = 0.64, p = 0.00005 …) and no significant difference between the estimated depth values (mean difference = 271 ± 49 μm SEM; p = 0.98, Wilcoxon signed-rank test)". A 271 µm *mean* alongside p = 0.98 suggests the 271 µm is a mean *absolute* difference; this is unverified. Also: "14/34 … 200 μm or more disagreement" and "phase reversal 600 μm below the CSD estimate" in one example. On drift: "Drift of 100–300 μm is consistently observed across recordings", and "the estimate of input layer depth consistently drifted deeper across recording sessions by an average of 132 ± 26 μm (SEM)" (first vs last 2 min). The SPI pattern held under re-referencing to the shallowest contact, the deepest contact or a common average, and across bands from 4–8 to 30–50 Hz. — [Davis et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.84512)
- **Mendoza-Halliday 2024 (crossover and CSD vs electrolytic-lesion histology).** "The mean distance of the crossover channel to layer 4 was 58 μm for LIP and 27 μm for LPFC (95% confidence interval (CI) (−19 μm, 135 μm) for LIP and (−183 μm, 237 μm) for LPFC)." "The median laminar position of the early CSD sink was 4.0, the same as that of the alpha-beta/gamma crossover … However, the 95% CI of the mean laminar position of the CSD early sink across probes (between 3.6 and 4.67, n = 14) was significantly larger than that of the crossover (between 3.82 and 4.45, n = 24) … (Ansari–Bradley … P = 0.04)." Only "n = 3 probes in V1" had histology. FLIP gave "less than 200 μm error" with ≥25 s of data. — [Mendoza-Halliday et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01554-7)
- **Mackey et al. critique (PP 2024; Nat Neurosci 2026)** [summary; verify against the published version]. On their A1, belt and V1 data, "the crossover fell in L4 in 29% of cases overall (A1 32%, belt 40%, V1 8%)" with FLIP, and "In 71% of cases, the FLIP crossover fell in the infragranular layers". With vFLIP, L4 was identified in 42% for V1. FLIP and vFLIP crossovers "agreed in 49% of cases within 200 µm". Monopolar (distant) referencing inflates false positives through volume conduction, and they recommend bipolar, CSD or Laplacian derivations. — [Mackey et al. 2024](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.18.613490v1.full)
- **Zhang/Li/Callaway 2024 (PP; 20 µm Neuropixels plus histology, anaesthetised V1).** CSD profiles "are low resolution and inconsistent across both penetrations and visual stimulus conditions, making it impossible to summarize a stereotypical CSD profile". A sink-source boundary drawn from 100 µm data "might deviate substantially" from the one seen at 20 µm. They do not give a single mean error in µm in the parts I read. — [Zhang, Li & Callaway 2024](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.01.23.576944)
- **Dougherty 2019** quotes its own alignment resolution as "0.1 mm +/− 0.05 mm" [summary]. — [Dougherty et al. 2019](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/320218v2.full)

### Inferences
- With 100 µm arrays a realistic alignment error for one penetration is about ±0.1–0.3 mm, and the CSD sink is the noisier marker against histology (Mendoza-Halliday). Disagreements of 0.4–0.6 mm happen occasionally (Davis's 600 µm example). **Disagreements near 1–1.5 mm are larger than all of L4C plus L5/6 (~0.77 mm)** and so cannot be ordinary alignment noise. They point to one marker sitting on a different structure: a second fold or sheet, white matter, or a spurious or non-geniculate "earliest" sink. Alternatively a spectral or phase gradient is driven by volume conduction (Mackey) or by drift (Davis).
- None of the three markers is histologically validated in V1 at better than about 0.2 mm, and the spectral crossover is actively contested in V1. A defensible anchor is therefore the **sink bottom (L4C/L5)**, the most-used and most-cited convention with the classic Mitzdorf/Schroeder histology. Spike-phase and the PSD should serve as consistency checks, with an explicit tolerance of about 0.2–0.3 mm, about the inter-marker disagreement reported by Davis and Mendoza-Halliday. Penetrations outside that tolerance should be flagged, or excluded in a sensitivity analysis, not averaged.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024–2026 head-to-head study in **awake macaque V1** that compares sink, crossover and spike-phase against histology with mean disagreement in µm. I did not read the published 2026 Mackey Matters Arising or any Bastos reply, so the Mackey V1 numbers above are preprint values obtained through a summary.

## 5. Continuous-depth models and robustness of laminar conclusions to the reference

### Takeaway
Few papers model depth continuously or test whether conclusions survive a change of reference. Mostly they assert robustness: Maier 2010 says "results do not critically depend on this approximation". The concrete examples are Davis 2023, which realigned the 14 discordant sessions by each marker; Westerberg 2023, which ran a supplementary analysis with a fixed 5-channel window; and Zhu/Moore, which assign compartments per session from histological thicknesses instead of fixed 0.5 mm windows.

### Cited findings
- Maier 2010: "considered the zone ±200 μm … to be the approximate extent of layer 4, though the results do not critically depend on this approximation." — [Maier et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnsys.2010.00031)
- Davis 2023 measured "the average firing rate as a function of depth relative to either the CSD or LPC estimate of the input layer" in the N = 14 sessions that disagreed by >200 µm (Fig. 6a). They also tested re-referencing and frequency bands. — [Davis et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.84512)
- Westerberg 2023 used an alternative supplementary definition of the input sink: "the average of 5 recording channels immediately above the L4/5 boundary". — [Westerberg et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-41745-1)
- Carr et al. 2025 (PP) "scaled [depths] to average layer boundaries across the five sessions", a piecewise-linear depth normalisation, and report neuron depth as a continuous variable (for example, a mean distance from layer 4 of 304 µm, 95% CI 266–342 µm) [summary]. — [Carr et al. 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.05.14.653875v1.full)
- Cox 2019 and Dougherty 2019 plot CSD and MUA at 0.1 mm resolution relative to L4/5 as continuous depth profiles, alongside the compartment statistics. — [Cox et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1167/19.12.13)

### Inferences
- Reporting effects as a function of continuous depth relative to the anchor (for example a smooth or GAM over depth, with penetration as a random effect) avoids committing to the uncertain 0.5 mm compartment edges. Re-running the analysis under each marker's alignment would make laminar claims auditable. Two things support this. The typical inter-marker error (about 0.2–0.3 mm) is similar to the width of 4C itself. And the labs' compartment edges already differ by up to about 0.25 mm.

### Gaps
- I found no published V1 study that systematically re-runs its main laminar conclusion under several alternative anchors (sink bottom vs crossover vs spike-phase) and reports how often the conclusion survives. Davis 2023 Fig. 6 comes closest, and its result was not captured in my extract.
