# Anchor V1 depth at the 4C/5 border

In macaque V1 the earliest visually evoked current sink lies in layer 4C. **No published study has measured, against histology, how far below it the 4C/5 border lies.** Every number in circulation is either a lab convention or geometry. The one modern thickness set calibrated by histology puts 4C at **281 µm** and layers 5–6 together at **489 µm** ([Zhu et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12774416/)). On those numbers the border lies about 0 µm below the bottom of a sink that fills 4C, about 140 µm below the centre of such a sink, about 210 µm below the centre of a sink confined to 4Cα, and about 280 µm below the top of 4C. The project's own median sink-to-flip offset of +0.3 mm sits at the deep end of that range, which fits an onset-defined sink at the top of 4C (our inference). The two other markers have no V1 histology of a usable size. The spike-phase flip was validated against the CSD in V4, MT and PFC only ([Davis et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10162800)). The spectral crossing rests on 3–5 lesioned V1 probes from one monkey ([Mendoza-Halliday et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10917659/)), and an independent V1 test found it in layer 4 in only **8%** of fitted penetrations ([Mackey et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11429918)). The earliest spikes come from two depths, 4Cα and layer 6, plus afferent axons in the white matter, so "earliest MUA" alone cannot locate the 4C/5 border. Monkey J's earliest MUA, 0.3–0.7 mm below the flip, falls where layer 6 and the grey–white transition should be if the flip is right. If the flip were instead that far off, the sink 0.55–1.05 mm above it would be even further from 4C, so in those sessions the sink has failed whichever reading holds. The recommendations are to keep the 4C/5 border as the anchor target and the cross-checked flip as its primary estimate, to add two plausibility gates based on anatomical thickness, to describe the input band honestly as 4C plus 4B, and to shorten and split the deep band, because its last 0.1–0.25 mm probably lies in white matter.

## No study measured the sink-to-4C/5 distance against histology

The claim that the sink rule is "histologically verified" traces back to one lineage. Schroeder, Vaughan and colleagues reconstructed tracks and lesions in awake macaques. What they validated was the depths at which VEP components invert, not the edge of a sink. Kraut and colleagues put the flash N40 sinks in **4Cβ** and N24 in 4A ([Kraut et al. 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2408876/)). Schroeder et al. 1991 placed the pattern-reversal N50 "primarily" in 4C and did not name a sublayer ([Schroeder et al. 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1891808/)). The 1998 synthesis set the array relative to "the base of lamina 4c as defined by the inversion point of the flash-evoked N40 component". It called internal laminar assignments "approximate" and reported no offsets in µm between the sink and the anatomy ([Schroeder et al. 1998](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~msereno/569/readings/CSD-visualareas.pdf)). Anaesthetised studies with histology checked something different again. Smith and colleagues placed lesions at the array ends and found them "at the expected locations" ([Smith et al. 2013](https://jiaxx.github.io/assets/img/2012%20Laminar%20dependence%20of%20neuronal%20correlations%20in%20visual%20cortex.pdf)). Bijanzadeh and colleagues used DiI and cytochrome oxidase to confirm verticality, then called every border other than 4C "necessarily tentative" ([Bijanzadeh et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279245/)). Zhu and colleagues took the sink-source reversal as the 4C/5 border, adjusted it by up to ±1 channel group from histology, and gave no discrepancy statistic ([Zhu et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12774416/)). The Maier lineage, which includes the lab whose monkeys B and J may be the animals in this dataset, used MRI and no histology ([Cox et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6797477/)).

Which sublayer the sink occupies is also contested, and the popular "4Cα" label is weaker than its citations suggest. Maier et al. wrote that the sink "corresponds most closely to layer 4Cα" ([Maier et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2928665/)). Their source is electrical stimulation, which put fast-afferent monosynaptic activity in 4Cα **and layer 6** and slow-afferent activity in lower 4Cβ and layer 6 ([Mitzdorf & Singer 1979](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/114553/)). The lesion lineage put the flash sink in 4Cβ instead ([Kraut et al. 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2408876/)), and Schroeder's summary of Givre's colour study describes separate upper (M) and lower (P) 4C sinks ([Schroeder et al. 1998](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~msereno/569/readings/CSD-visualareas.pdf)). At 100 µm spacing the early sink typically spans one to four contacts, about 200–400 µm ([Smith et al. 2013](https://jiaxx.github.io/assets/img/2012%20Laminar%20dependence%20of%20neuronal%20correlations%20in%20visual%20cortex.pdf); [Hansen et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3653617/)). At 20 µm the strong short-latency sink spans "only … about 200µm, far narrower than L4C". There, the old sink-to-source criterion would have put the bottom of 4C at an implausible 1800 µm in one penetration, and a black-to-white flip can leave a "zero latency sink" in 4Cβ ([Zhang, Li & Callaway 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)). The safest reading at our resolution is "the sink is in 4C, weighted toward whichever stream the stimulus favours". A grating is a different stimulus from the flashes most of this literature used.

The distance to the border therefore depends on which part of the sink is used and which sublayer the sink fills. The table separates what was measured from what was agreed.

Two processing facts move these numbers. Smoothing widens a sink symmetrically, so its centroid stays put while its bottom edge moves deeper by up to about half the kernel width. Published kernels range from σ = 30 µm ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)) to σ = 180 µm ([Cox et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6797477/)), and van Kerkoerle used a 200 µm differentiation step ([van Kerkoerle et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5227065/)). The centroid is therefore the landmark more robust to processing, and the bottom edge the more sensitive one (our inference from convolution geometry). The project's sink marker is onset-defined: "earliest", not "strongest". M afferents end in 4Cα ([Blasdel & Lund 1983](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6564440/)), and 4Cα responds first ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/); [Nowak et al. 1995](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/visual-neuroscience/article/visual-latencies-in-areas-v1-and-v2-of-the-macaque-monkey/2E43515431CBCFCC2451939F734E3427)). An onset-defined sink should therefore sit in the upper half of 4C, 140–280 µm above the border. The project's empirical +0.3 mm median falls at the top of that range, within one contact of it. It is a project measurement and is consistent with the anatomy. It has not been validated against histology.

## The layer budget: about 1.25 mm above the 4C/5 border and 0.5 mm below

Total thickness sets the strongest constraint the literature offers, because it bounds where any border can sit. In vivo, V1 measured about **1.5–2.0 mm** pia to white matter on Neuropixels, varying with coverslip compression and swelling, and thinner toward the V1/V2 border ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)). Fixed opercular (central) cortex measured about **2.26 mm** and calcarine (peripheral) cortex about **1.65 mm**, with no stated shrinkage correction ([Ahmed et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13399498/)). In 2–5° opercular V1, the eccentricity of this project, processing shrank tissue by **11% along the depth axis** ([Kelly & Hawken 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5572544/)). Fixed depths should therefore be scaled up by about 1.1–1.2 before they are compared with recordings (inference). A working prior for a parafoveal awake penetration is 1.7–2.0 mm. Ahmed's 2.26 mm is an upper bound, since young animals, foveal sampling or oblique sections may inflate it.

Within that column, the compartment thicknesses below come from Zhu's histology-offset CSD scheme, Zhang's rules and Ahmed's layer 5. The depths relative to the 4C/5 border are our assembly of those sources (inference, ±15–20% per boundary).

Independent physiology agrees with this budget. In awake Maier-lab recordings, the bottom of layer 4 sat on average **1.2 mm below the top of V1 and 0.5 mm above its bottom** ([Cox et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6797477/)). Bijanzadeh's penetrations put the bottom of 4C 0.95–1.1 mm below the top of a 1.5–1.7 mm cortex ([Bijanzadeh et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6279245/)). Summing Zhu's compartments gives 1.24 mm from the top to the 4C/5 border. The project's median distance from the evoked-MUA top to the v3 anchor is **1.25 mm**, an excellent match ([docs/09](../docs/09_paper_design.md)). Its interquartile range of **0.80–1.52 mm** matters just as much. A distance of 0.8 mm is shorter than L2/3 plus 4A/B (≈ 0.96 mm) even before L1 is counted. At least a quarter of the penetrations therefore have an anchor that sits too high, a top that sits too low, or superficial compression during insertion. Zhang and colleagues raise that last possibility explicitly and recommend deep anchors over pia-based ones for that reason ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)).

Geometry adds two smaller corrections. Thickness measured along an oblique probe is the true thickness divided by cos θ: +6% at 20° and +15% at 30° (arithmetic). A 0.49 mm L5–6 therefore reads as 0.52 mm at 20° and 0.57 mm at 30°. On a 100 µm pitch, 4Cβ spans one or two contacts and 5Aα less than one, so boundaries cannot be placed more finely than about ±50 µm before any anatomical uncertainty is added ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)).

## The phase flip and spectral crossing have almost no V1 histology

The spike-phase flip was **defined against the sink**, not against anatomy. Davis and colleagues took "the bottom of the current sink" as their CSD reference. They chose the laminar-phase-coupling boundary by eye, blind to the CSD, in 34 sessions from macaque V4 and marmoset MT and PFC. **None was in V1, and none had histology** ([Davis et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10162800)). The two estimates correlated (r = 0.64) and showed no mean bias. In **14 of 34** sessions they still disagreed by 200 µm or more, up to about 600 µm. In those sessions the flip tracked the low/high-frequency power inversion better than the CSD did. The flip also drifted **132 ± 26 µm** deeper between the first and last two minutes of a recording. The phase step was about 1.3–1.4 rad, not a full π. The reported "271 ± 49 µm" next to p = 0.98 is probably a mean absolute difference, which we have not verified. By construction, the flip therefore targets the input/deep boundary, nominally 4C/5. Its closest V1 analogue is the sharp superficial/deep coherence transition "near the bottom of layer 4", about one contact below the sink centre ([Maier et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2928665/)). In the project the flip sits a median 0.3 mm below the earliest sink. That fits the 140–280 µm expected from the sink to 4C/5 if the flip marks the border. No V1 study tests this.

The alpha-beta/gamma crossing claims more and supports less in V1. Mendoza-Halliday and colleagues state that the crossover "corresponded to layer 4". In example probes it lay "in layer 4 or the layer 4-to-5 boundary", and the pooled unipolar distance was **46 ± 51 µm (SEM) from the centre of layer 4**. The lesion validation, however, was mostly in LIP and LPFC. V1 contributed **3 probes according to the text and 5 according to a figure legend**, all from one monkey, with no V1-specific distance. The authors themselves flag that V1 had its highest alpha-beta power in white matter, "likely due to volume conduction" from V2 ([Mendoza-Halliday et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10917659/)). An independent dataset defined layer 4 by the sink and MUA. In it, FLIP fitted only 50% of V1 penetrations and put the crossing in layer 4 in **8%**; 71% of crossings across all areas were infragranular, and two V1 fits came out inverted ([Mackey et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11429918)). The rebuttal finds the motif by eye in 16 of 24 of Mackey's V1 probes. It reports its revised crossover **80 µm** from histological layer 4 (95% CI −92 to +260 µm, n = 18, areas pooled) and calls sink-based ground truth "circular" ([Major et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12247776/)). Both sides published in *Nature Neuroscience* in December 2025 ([The Transmitter 2025](https://www.thetransmitter.org/brain-waves/dispute-erupts-over-universal-cortical-brain-wave-claim/)). The Mackey numbers above come from the preprint, read through a summary. Earlier V1 work found gamma about 50% stronger above the bottom of layer 4 but **no laminar difference at 5–20 Hz** ([Maier et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2928665/)).

Neither side resolves one ambiguity specific to V1: which "layer 4"? In most areas layer 4 is thin. In V1, 4A through 4C spans about 0.6 mm, so its centre would sit near the 4B/4Cα junction, about 0.3 mm above the 4C/5 border. A crossing at the "centre of layer 4" would therefore sit above the flip. The V1 critiques, the white-matter alpha-beta and the project's own +0.1 mm offset all place it at or near the border instead (our inference). The crossing is best used as a coarse superficial-versus-deep orientation check. Its V1 error bars are wider than 4C itself.

| Marker | Nominal anatomical target | V1 validation | Typical disagreement | Status of the target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Earliest sink (onset) | 4C, probably weighted to 4Cα | Lesion lineage (VEP inversions); no µm offsets | Alignment "within 200 µm" (Maier); old sink rule unreliable at 20 µm (Zhang) | **Convention, backed qualitatively by histology** |
| Spike-phase flip | Input/deep boundary (≈ 4C/5), defined as the sink bottom | None (V4, MT, PFC only) | ≥ 200 µm in 41% of sessions; drift 132 µm | **Convention, inherited from the CSD** |
| Alpha-beta/gamma crossing | "Layer 4" (centre) | 3–5 probes from one monkey; 8% in L4 in an independent V1 set | CI −92 to +260 µm (pooled) | **Contested** |

## Earliest spikes mark two depths, 4Cα and layer 6

The latency literature consistently names two early depths, not one. The shortest-latency units in each animal (20–31 ms) were "all found in layers 4C or 6" ([Maunsell & Gibson 1992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1432087/), abstract read through a secondary index). In all Neuropixels penetrations "the responses in L4Cα have the shortest latency", and one example ran 4Cα, then 6A, then 4A, then 4Cβ ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)). In awake macaques with flashed gratings, the onset order ran from the input layers, 4C and 6, to the output layers. On a relative fast-suppression latency, 4Cα (11.4 ms) and L6 (14.9 ms) came first and **4Cβ came last (21.3 ms)** ([Wang et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7511183/)). The first feedforward MUA appeared "in layers 4C and 6" ([van Kerkoerle et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5227065/)). 4Cβ lagged 4Cα by about 20 ms for flashed spots under anaesthesia ([Nowak et al. 1995](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/visual-neuroscience/article/visual-latencies-in-areas-v1-and-v2-of-the-macaque-monkey/2E43515431CBCFCC2451939F734E3427)). In the LGN, the fastest M cells led the fastest P cells by about 10 ms ([Maunsell et al. 1999](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/visual-neuroscience/article/abs/visual-response-latencies-of-magnocellular-and-parvocellular-lgn-neurons-in-macaque-monkeys/8AB0A24431EF71A91229FA28E272EC12)). Supragranular layers trail 4C by about 8–15 ms ([Schroeder et al. 1998](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~msereno/569/readings/CSD-visualareas.pdf)). With gratings in awake monkeys, the granular-to-infragranular lag shrank from about 7 ms for small stimuli to about 2 ms for large ones ([Gieselmann & Thiele 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8916775/)).

Anatomy explains the second early band. Geniculate afferents to the upper half of 4Cα send "arborizing collaterals in layer 6B", and layer-1 afferents send collaterals to 6A ([Blasdel & Lund 1983](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6564440/)). Reviews add sparse M collaterals to lower layer 6 and P collaterals to upper layer 6 ([Vanni et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7233004/); [Briggs & Usrey 2011](https://www.cns.nyu.edu/~tony/vns/readings/briggs-usrey-2011.pdf)). No reviewed source supports direct geniculate input to layer 5. The white matter is a third source. It carries "multiunit activity in the presence of a near-zero second derivative", and MUA "often extends considerably beyond" the grey–white boundary ([Schroeder et al. 1998](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~msereno/569/readings/CSD-visualareas.pdf)). Inverted, axonal spikes mark the L6/WM border on Neuropixels, and some appear in 4Cβ, where P afferents are dense ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)). Positive-going waveforms are more common in white matter ([PNAS 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12132580/); authors not captured). Single-axon work in rabbit and cat shows that thalamic terminals fire about 0.7 ms before their sink, in layer 4 and sometimes in layer 6 ([Swadlow et al. 2002](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6757983); [Jin et al. 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2556869)). No macaque study measures how much afferent spiking advances intact-cortex MUA by depth.

A latency profile alone therefore cannot place the 4C/5 border. Zhang and colleagues use latency only for the 4Cα/4Cβ split and set the 4C/5 border from AP-band power and coherence ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)). No paper reports a µm error for any latency-defined boundary. The usable form is a profile. Going down the probe, the expected sequence is early 4Cα, later 4Cβ, a late layer 5, early layer 6, and white matter with early MUA but a flat CSD. The 4C/5 border then sits about one 4Cβ thickness (≈ 140 µm) below the top of the late trough (inference). At 100 µm pitch, a trough one to three contacts wide can vanish under smoothing and noise.

These facts change how the monkey J result should be read. In 7 J penetrations the earliest sink sits 0.55–1.05 mm above the flip in 6 and 0.75 mm below it in 1. The earliest MUA sits 0.3–0.7 mm below the flip, the onset falls steadily down the probe, and deep sites lead the input band by about 2–3 ms ([docs/09, check AC](../docs/09_paper_design.md)). Suppose first that the flip sits at 4C/5. Then 0.3–0.5 mm below it is layer 6, including the 6B target of M collaterals, and 0.5–0.7 mm is the grey–white transition, where afferent axons are recorded. Both are depths the literature expects to be early. The input band would then contain 4Cβ and 4B as well as 4Cα, and the literature puts 4Cβ late. A deep lead of 2–3 ms over the band median therefore needs no misplacement. On this reading the sink, 0.55–1.05 mm above the flip, lies in 4A/4B or lower layer 3 rather than 4C. Sinks there are known for S-cone stimuli, in 4A and 3B ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849622/)). Suppose instead that the flip is 0.3–0.7 mm too high and the deep early band is 4Cα. Then the true 4C/5 border lies another ~0.15–0.3 mm deeper, and the sink would sit 1.0–2.0 mm above it, farther than all of L2/3 plus layer 4. **Under neither reading can these sinks be in 4C.** The sink fails in these sessions either way; whether the flip also fails is what the distance checks below decide (our inference). The two readings make different predictions about distances already on the cards. Under the first, the flip lies about 1.1–1.4 mm below the evoked-MUA top and about 0.45–0.6 mm above the end of grey matter. Under the second, the flip lies only about 0.5–1.0 mm below the top.

Against the layer budget, the conventional input window of 0 to +0.5 mm covers all of 4C (0 to +0.28) and most of 4B. That matters for a binocular study, because 4B and 4Cβ differ in their input stream. The conventional 0.5 mm deep window roughly matches layers 5–6. The project's 0.7 mm cap reaches 0.1–0.25 mm past the expected grey–white border on a perpendicular penetration. Only at about 30° obliquity does it stay inside grey matter (inference). Inter-lab definitions of "deep" already differ by about 0.25 mm before any alignment error. Two labs leave guard gaps at the boundary (van Kerkoerle ±0.05 mm, Cox ±0.1 mm), which concedes that the contacts touching the anchor are ambiguous. Exclusion is the usual remedy for bad penetrations. Penetrations without a clear sink, without overlapping receptive fields across depth, or with too little coverage are dropped. The Maier lab's V1 study kept 27 of 44 penetrations (8 B, 19 J). It required at least 0.5 mm per compartment and used the spectral gradient to confirm the first fold of cortex ([Carlson et al. 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.04.680461v1.full), preprint read through a summary; that these are the same animals is unverified). Across studies, the reported disagreement between markers is typically 0.1–0.3 mm ([Davis et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10162800); [Mendoza-Halliday et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10917659/)). A disagreement of 1–1.5 mm exceeds 4C plus layers 5–6 combined (≈ 0.77 mm). It means one marker sits on a different structure, not that the markers carry noise.

## Recommendations for anchor, compartments and checks

**Anchor target.** Keep the 4C/5 (input/deep) border. It is the dominant convention and the definition the flip inherits. It also sits mid-column, safe from the superficial compression that affects pia-based anchors. **Anchor estimate.** Keep v3's cross-checked flip as primary and the +0.3 mm sink offset (project-measured, inside the 0.14–0.28 mm geometric prior plus one contact). Two changes are justified. First, stop letting the crossing alone confirm a flip unless the anatomical gates below also pass, because the crossing is the least-validated marker in V1. Second, add the two thickness gates as formal flags. v3's 0.4 mm agreement tolerance is wider than 4C. Literature tolerances run 0.2–0.3 mm, so report 0.3 mm as a sensitivity analysis rather than rewriting the frozen rule.

The 7 discordant J penetrations should be run through the first two checks before anything else. Those checks discriminate between "flip right, sink in upper layers" and "both high" with no new analysis assumptions. Two further tests discriminate on the spikes themselves. The first is unit waveform polarity at the earliest deep contacts, since positive or triphasic waveforms indicate axons. The second is a within-band latency step at +0.05 to +0.15 mm, where 4Cβ should lag 4Cα. A third test is not covered by the reviewed literature. It is the 45% versus 90% contrast-latency shift by depth, on the general expectation that M-recipient sites shift less than P-recipient sites. The ocularity profile is similar: in a binocular dataset, monocularity should peak in 4C. That expectation also comes from outside these notes and should be cited separately if used.

## Conclusion

The literature does not give this project a histologically measured anchor. It gives a thickness budget, and that budget is the more useful constraint. Any anchor must leave about 1.1–1.4 mm of cortex above it and about 0.5 mm below it, and the three markers can be tested against those distances penetration by penetration. The budget also reframes the monkey J puzzle. Early deep spiking 0.3–0.7 mm below a correct 4C/5 border is what the layer 6 and white-matter literature predicts, and a deep lead over an input band that contains late 4Cβ needs no misplacement. Sinks 0.55–1.05 mm above the flip, on the other hand, cannot be 4C sinks under any reading. In those sessions the sink is the marker that has certainly failed; the flip is innocent until the top-to-anchor distance says otherwise. Two corrections follow for the paper. The deep group's last 0.1–0.25 mm probably samples the grey–white transition, so effects described as "deep-layer" should be shown to hold within 0.5 mm, or in layer 6 separately. And "input" should be described as 4C plus 4B, because a 0.5 mm window is about 1.8 times the thickness of 4C. Neither correction changes the frozen v3 analysis, and both are cheap to report.
